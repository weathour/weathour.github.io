On the day *Kaiou GRAPH* went on sale, Setsuna saw one name printed in the largest type on its cover: Kazusa Touma. Inside was Haruki's exclusive interview, accompanied by photographs he had taken on the streets of Strasbourg.

Haruki had already wondered during proofreading whether Setsuna might recognize the location. He removed the cathedral and other distinctive streets, omitted the place of the interview, and left only the copy and photographs needed for publication. The feature did not invent the interview or falsify Kazusa's career. Haruki reduced the clues from which Setsuna could reconstruct what had happened. The magazine still confirmed for her that he had concealed the meeting.[^report]

Setsuna does not ask why he saw Kazusa. She first repeats something she has said before: "I won't ask you not to see her." Then she names what hurt her. She wanted to know before the meeting.

The feature gives Coda's problem a tangible form. Concealment passes through photography, revision, proofreading, and publication until it appears on paper. When Haruki removes the landmarks, he also decides when Setsuna can know and how much she can learn.

[The previous essay, "Three Years Without a Piano"](/en/posts/white-album-2-three-years-without-piano/) described how CC lets Kazusa speak again in Strasbourg after her long absence. The interview assignment then guarantees that she and Haruki will meet in a professional setting. By Coda, the third person is no longer waiting outside the relationship. Kazusa is an interview subject, a professional pianist, Youko's daughter, and someone Haruki repeatedly cares for. Setsuna works at a record company, remains Haruki's lover, and wears a ring that draws the Ogiso family into their engagement. All three now have concrete jobs. Whenever they meet, someone must change copy, travel plans, medical arrangements, or concert schedules, and both families may be drawn in.

![Youko and Kazusa stand together for an interview, with Haruki outside the frame.](./white-album-2-third-person-returns/coda-interview-kazusa-yoko.jpg)

*Kazusa re-enters Haruki's work as the subject of an interview. The frame shows Youko and Kazusa; Haruki, asking the questions, remains outside it. PS3 gameplay frame: ©2012 AQUAPLUS / via xueyinhualuo, Bilibili.*

Discussing Coda, Fumiaki Maruto places "adult decisions" among such practical acts as resigning, handing over work, and saying goodbye. A person may abandon an existing life, but must explain the decision to colleagues and loved ones, complete the necessary handover, and refrain from shifting responsibility afterward.[^creator]

But when is a choice complete? When someone recognizes whom he loves, when the player presses the final line, or when that line passes through a breakup, resignation, wedding, and migration? If the people affected hear only an edited account, or if repair always falls to the person best able to endure, then part of the choice remains unfinished even after it has been carried out.

The four endings divide along that fracture: who acknowledges desire, who acts on it, when the people affected learn the facts, whether their answer can alter the outcome, and who inherits the aftermath. Coda never gives all these acts to one person. Each route proves one thing and leaves another undone.

*This essay contains full spoilers for Coda's four endings and makes a limited comparison with the opening of Yasunari Kawabata's Snow Country. The PS3 story 不倶戴天の君へ and the two True after stories are reserved for the next essay. The cover is an original conceptual image. ## By the Time the Button Appears, the Choice Has Already Begun

Coda opens by giving Haruki and Setsuna each a business card. Haruki works at Kaiou Publishing, where he conducts interviews, proofreads, and plans magazine issues. Setsuna works in publicity at a record company. They exchange cards in a conference room, then discuss travel and a proposal over the weekend. Their jobs authorize them to arrange trips, take over interviews, select photographs, and approach artists on behalf of an institution.

The Strasbourg reunion grows from those arrangements. The assigned journalist hands the trip to Haruki at short notice. Setsuna arrives in Europe as promised to join him. Kazusa spends a holiday there each year, and Youko's agency has accepted the interview. Three schedules meet on Christmas Eve. Travel, leave, and professional work have prepared what looks like a chance reunion.

Haruki first helps an injured woman on the street and stays after recognizing Kazusa. He keeps her warm, treats her foot, and carries her. He eases the injured woman's pain. He can also justify staying through common decency, responsibility, and the refusal to abandon someone in the snow. Those reasons let him approach someone he has long wanted to approach.[^care]

Setsuna knows only that Haruki is caring for "that person," so she praises him for doing the right thing. Haruki knows that he has broken their promise to meet within a week and that the injured woman is Kazusa. Kazusa knows Setsuna is waiting and still asks him to stay. Setsuna can judge the care only from the abridged account she receives. Haruki cares for Kazusa while also deciding which version Setsuna will use to judge him.

Back in Japan, Haruki tells Setsuna that he met Kazusa, then stops halfway and decides that he has "said enough." Setsuna wants the three of them to meet again, yet she also feels jealous and afraid and doubts how sincere that wish can be. She has never demanded that Haruki stop seeing Kazusa forever. She asks him to tell her beforehand, so that she can be angry and decide whether she wants to take part.

The feature then turns the concealment into a magazine. Haruki removes the landmarks and hopes Kazusa will have left Japan by publication day, sparing him another explanation. What Setsuna reads is Haruki's edited version of the past, prepared for publication before it reaches her.

Youko later expands one interview into a month-long exclusive project and tells Haruki to reject Kazusa himself. Haruki calls Setsuna while she is rehearsing. He hopes she will blame him or cry, giving him a reason to refuse the assignment. She never answers.[^assignment]

Haruki first waits for Setsuna to veto the job for him. When the call fails, he accepts and calls that choice "taking responsibility himself." The menu no longer asks whether he will accept. Both options lead to the assignment; the player can decide only whether Haruki admits that he is doing it for Kazusa.

Later menus keep asking how he will name his actions. When he misses Setsuna's performance, he may admit that he "won't go" or say that he "can't go." When Kazusa leans against him, he may "hold her tight" or recast the same embrace as merely offering her his chest. Each press lets the player choose the words Haruki will use for his own action.

The exclusive interview also tangles professional confidentiality with private concealment. Kazusa gets a ticket and attends Setsuna's performance alone. She sees the light onstage and knows that three years of tears lie behind it. She does not go backstage. Instead, she invokes the interview's confidentiality to make Haruki hide her visit from Setsuna. A rule meant to protect unpublished work now shuts Setsuna out of a relationship that directly concerns her.

After Kazusa's own performance fails, she slaps Haruki, presses closer, and threatens him with a kiss. Attack, jealousy, and a plea for closeness arrive together. She finally proposes a limited arrangement: Setsuna will receive real love, Kazusa false love, and the arrangement will last only through the winter.

Haruki knows that words of love, mutual embraces, and sex cannot remain separate from feeling merely because he calls them "false." He says he must decide whom to hurt, then blames the confusion on a fever and admits that he still refuses to choose. The game remembers how he answered earlier: where his disclosure stopped, whether he admitted approaching Kazusa of his own accord, and how much Setsuna knows. Earlier answers darken later options or remove a whole menu. The names he has already given his actions become the limits of what he can say next.

The final menu does not place the two women's names side by side. Its options read "I love Setsuna" and "I can't lie to Kazusa." The first confirms a love that still exists. The second admits that Haruki can no longer call his feelings for Kazusa a performance. Both may be true, but the player can choose only which one he acts on first. Setsuna has already demanded knowledge and drawn back. Kazusa has performed, avoided a meeting, and proposed a winter-long affair. Haruki has accepted the interview, edited the feature, cared for Kazusa, and concealed what happened. By the time the final button appears, all three have made a series of choices. The button drives those acts toward different endings.

Coda has four endings without offering a four-way menu. "Let go" locks in the Affair route. Haruki must reject the false love that night before the final two statements can appear. Earlier disclosures and actions determine whether they appear and where they lead. The other three routes first deal with Kazusa's injured hands, the special issue, Youko's illness, and the engagement, then divide into Coda Normal, Kazusa True, and Setsuna True.[^routes]

The menus first rule out the simplest account of choice: a pure act of will does not suddenly emerge from Haruki's mind at the final screen. How he has cared, concealed, and disclosed determines what he can still say. The player presses one sentence; the route settles everything that made that sentence available.

## Choosing in Private Does Not Mean Letting Others Hear

### The Three Non-Affair Routes First Pass Through the Same Stretch of Life

When Haruki refuses "a winter of false love," Kazusa has already cut her right hand on glass from the window she smashed, and she sprains her left wrist that night. He reports both injuries to the editorial office as a fall down the stairs after the performance. The broken window, the argument, the confession, and their time alone in the room never enter the report. His colleagues revise the schedule from the account he gives them.

Haruki remains in Kazusa's room. He cooks, changes her dressings, helps her bathe, feeds her, and holds her hand at night. Kazusa first retracts her confession as feverish nonsense, then says that fever makes it harder to lie. Both know the confession has not vanished, but neither has to answer at once. The wound needs care each day, allowing today to continue into tomorrow.

Haruki first attributes his stay to the injured hands, Youko's disappearance, and his own responsibility. The narration speaks more plainly: Kazusa is not refusing to let him go; Haruki is refusing to let go of Kazusa. Her injuries require care, and they also give him daily entry to her room, a reason to arrange her meals, and contact with her body. He solves an immediate problem for Kazusa while postponing his own answer.

One interview soon grows into a special issue of more than one hundred pages devoted to Kazusa Touma, the first issue Haruki has overseen from start to finish. He contacts the agency, controls the schedule, and selects interviews and photographs. Kazusa returns to the agency, apologizes to the staff, resumes practice, and decides to proceed with the additional concert.

Haruki then learns that Youko has leukemia. He begins arranging transport, treatment, and daily care, and agrees with Youko to hide the illness. They fear that Kazusa will collapse again before the concert, so they decide when she may learn about her mother's condition. Kazusa senses a secret but can only interpret it as Youko asking Haruki to remain out of pity. Haruki and Youko want to protect her performance, yet they also prevent her from deciding how to face her mother's illness.

Meanwhile, Setsuna's family discovers her ring. Through conversations in the Ogiso household and with Takeya and Io, a promise that has not been fully announced becomes an "engagement." Kazusa is beside Haruki when friends call to congratulate him. He says only, "Today, I'll look at you," explaining neither the engagement nor the illness. Kazusa slips away while he sleeps and goes straight to Youko. The secrecy finally breaks when Youko collapses in front of her daughter.[^normal]

These weeks draw all four into the same crisis without giving them the same facts. Haruki knows about both loves, the illness, the special issue, and the engagement. Youko consults him about when to reveal her condition. Kazusa and Setsuna can act only from accounts Haruki has cut short. The three non-Affair routes divide here, first testing whether a choice made in private is already enough.

### Coda Normal: Haruki Chooses in Private. Who Hears Him?

After that night, Haruki walks alone through the snow. He remembers how often he has asked someone else for an answer and says that he will ask no one again. This time he will choose for himself the person who matters most. The script does not name that person or show how he tells Setsuna and Kazusa. Kazusa True and Setsuna True continue with public disclosure and the other person's response. Coda Normal moves straight to one year later.

The earlier choices have already written this gap into the route. If Haruki has not preserved access to Setsuna True, especially if he did not tell Setsuna the truth about Kazusa in Osaka, pressing the same final option, "I love Setsuna," still leads to Coda Normal. The declaration may be sincere. Before speaking it, however, Haruki has not given Setsuna the full account of what happened with Kazusa. Setsuna may hear from someone else or see through him, but Haruki does not tell her in advance.

One year later, Haruki is succeeding at work. He and Setsuna are formally engaged and preparing for a June wedding. Their home, possible children, both families, and daily life have places in the plan. Kazusa cancels her additional concert and disappears from Japanese media. In the snow, Haruki admits that his feelings for her may last ten years, twenty years, or the rest of his life. He then returns to Setsuna and continues telling her that he loves her.

Haruki and Setsuna build a life from work, family, and daily companionship. Their love is real within this ending. Haruki speaks his other love only to the player. He chooses Setsuna in private, and the wedding, families, and work turn that choice into ordinary life. Kazusa leaves Japanese media during the missing year, while the script never shows Setsuna hearing the full story and answering it. The script does not say Haruki arranged Kazusa's disappearance. It does show a stable life that proceeds without Kazusa's presence or Setsuna's answer.[^normal]

The piano arrangement of *Shiawase na Kioku* accompanies ordinary streets and falling snow. The wedding has a date. Haruki's other love remains in a monologue spoken to the snow.

Coda Normal offers a first answer. Work, family, and the engagement can turn an inward choice into a stable life. It also leaves the first gap: stability can be established without full disclosure. Life has continued, but the people affected may not hear the whole story before the ending closes. If an orderly life can omit disclosure, can mutually acknowledged desire and a private life together restore what is missing?

### The Affair Route: Kazusa Sets the Bounds of One Winter, but No One Ends It Alone

Kazusa first defines the form of the Affair route. She leaves real love to Setsuna and asks Haruki only for one winter of "false love." She names the relationship and its deadline before he acts. The player then presses "Let go" for Haruki. He has been holding both of Kazusa's wrists to stop her from injuring her hands. Once he releases them, those hands are free to embrace and kiss him. Kazusa makes the proposal; Haruki turns it into bodily action and a route.

The option cannot always be selected. If Haruki told Setsuna the truth about Kazusa in Osaka, "Let go" is darkened. The Affair route requires that Haruki never made that disclosure. Setsuna may know Kazusa is present or see through Haruki, but he has not volunteered the full sequence or his motive. He and Kazusa can then organize the winter as a secret, calling their love pity, sex, fever, or a performance both know is incomplete.

"False love" supplies codes for contacting each other, dates for meetings, reasons to switch off their phones, and a deadline for leaving. They describe the agreement as lasting until winter and the concerts end. Yet they spend only a few days fully outside their ordinary schedules, living and traveling together, and the journey ends on the morning of its third day.[^affair] Haruki's love for Setsuna is also real, which makes it a cover for the arrangement. As long as he still loves Setsuna, he can tell himself that the relationship before him has not touched real love.

During their trip, the train enters a tunnel and the carriage goes dark. Haruki wakes Kazusa and asks whether she has read Yasunari Kawabata. White land opens outside after the train emerges, and the narration says that this is not the precise place described at the opening of *Snow Country*.[^snowcountry]

At the opening of *Snow Country*, Shimamura watches Yoko tend a sick man through a window that carries her reflection over the moving dusk. Intimacy first passes through an observer's gaze. Haruki wakes Kazusa, draws her close to the window, and waits with her for the white world. Soon afterward he says, "Kazusa decided; I only didn't object." He wakes her, shares the view, and continues the journey, yet phrases his consent as something that merely happened. He also calls the self who faces Setsuna, his colleagues, and the engagement a social performance, reserving "real" for the pair in the night.

The tunnel and blizzard close off the outside world for a while, so staying overnight can appear forced by the weather. At the inn, Haruki and Kazusa use false identities to get one room. They turn "we're about to marry," first used as a story for the inn clerk, into a private vow. Kazusa reaches toward Haruki. They call each other husband and wife and promise to remain together forever. The vow can be sincere that night. They do not tell Setsuna, and they make no plan for work, a home, their families, or life after winter.

![Kazusa, wearing a yukata and smiling through tears, extends both hands toward Haruki outside the frame.](./white-album-2-third-person-returns/coda-affair-private-vow.jpg)

*Within their winter-long relationship, the two make a vow witnessed only by each other; the frame contains no formal attire, rings, or other witnesses. PS3 gameplay frame: ©2012 AQUAPLUS / via xueyinhualuo, Bilibili.*

Kazusa had decided from the beginning that the journey would end there. Haruki returns to Setsuna and confesses the affair. Setsuna asks whether he ever thought of her. He answers, "I forgot you the whole time." The narration has already recorded Setsuna's voice entering the couple's private world more than once. His confession still contains a lie meant to punish himself. If he presents himself as a complete betrayer beyond rescue, he need not admit that he loves them both. He can also demand that Setsuna deny him another choice and make "going to hell alone" the only possible end.[^affair]

Haruki takes all the blame, yet he neither stops the affair nor repairs the life left behind. Kazusa says, "Let go." Haruki replies that he is not holding her: if she pulls her hand away, they will separate. He refuses to play the person who abandons her. Neither will make the final separation for the other, and at last their hands part. The script does not say who pulls away first. Kazusa set the deadline and announced her intention to end the relationship. She then delivers the broken Haruki to the woman she calls her sworn enemy. At the concert one year later, Haruki leaves early and Setsuna abandons her encore to follow him. The story then cuts back to the day one week after Kazusa's departure, when Setsuna enters Haruki's room and begins the year-long work of bringing him back to his job and daily life.

The order is clear. Kazusa defines the form and deadline, and Haruki agrees and makes the arrangement real in daily life. Kazusa announces the end, neither partner completes the separation alone, and Setsuna repairs the aftermath. Setsuna had no place in the winter agreement. A confession after the fact cannot return her to that earlier moment or let her decide whether to accept it. Haruki punishes himself most harshly; Kazusa largely controls the secret's duration, while Setsuna brings him back to ordinary life.

After Haruki rejects the secret affair, the other statement on the final menu reads, "I can't lie to Kazusa." It admits that he can no longer call his love for her a winter-long performance. Admitting his desire to himself is only the beginning. He must still tell Setsuna what happened and hear her answer.

The Affair route rules out another answer. Mutual love, shared life, and private vows still do not complete a choice. If the excluded person cannot refuse beforehand, and the ending and recovery are left to others, intense desire only defers the cost and passes it on.

## Kazusa True: Haruki Makes the Break. Who Answers for Setsuna?

Kazusa True turns Haruki's desire into public action. At its opening, Kazusa remains alone in a hotel suite and refuses to answer. Haruki lies that she left a "goodbye" message suggesting suicide, forcing the hotel staff to prepare to open the door. Before the key enters the lock, Kazusa opens it herself and exposes the lie. Haruki uses other people's fear of death to force a conversation, showing that he will no longer let Kazusa's silence end the night.

He proposes leaving with Kazusa, then asks whether she can accept a man who will betray his lover, friends, family, and work. Kazusa hears him handing off the cruelest decision and says that he has thrown the whole choice onto her. Five days later, she sends it back: betray Setsuna and choose me.

Haruki initiates their departure but wants Kazusa's signature on the final answer. She refuses to be merely rescued or to lighten his guilt.

Haruki then breaks up with Setsuna, resigns, hands over his work, faces Takeya's anger, explains himself to the Ogiso family, and prepares to leave Japan. Youko proposes that the three of them handle her illness together. Haruki refuses because that arrangement would prevent him from choosing Kazusa. The promises to stay, remain beside Setsuna forever, and marry her return one by one during the breakup. He may have meant them when he spoke them, but his later choice makes him responsible for breaking them aloud.[^kazusatrue]

Kazusa True draws its force from this sequence. Haruki does more than say that he chooses Kazusa. He carries the decision through company paperwork, farewells to family and friends, and migration to another country. He bears the social costs that he is able to bear.

Kazusa and Setsuna finally speak face to face. Kazusa admits that she is taking Haruki and that her jealousy makes life as a trio impossible. Setsuna asks whether Kazusa loves only an idealized Haruki. Kazusa offers to give up the piano as compensation and even tries to injure her hands. Setsuna rejects the exchange and stops her on instinct. Losing a career or a body cannot compensate for a relationship. Their hostility is still not deep enough for either woman to watch the other destroy herself.

Haruki breaks the engagement and leaves his friends, work, and life in Japan. Everyone can see the price he pays. Yet the version he gives Setsuna omits Youko's illness, and he repeatedly estimates how well she will endure: she has family, friends, and work, so she should survive better than Kazusa. Their support cannot give Setsuna the chance to hear the facts and answer for herself.

After leaving Japan, Haruki and Kazusa build a real new life. They work together in Vienna, care for each other through nightmares, and keep her piano career going. For two years they almost never mention Setsuna or Japan, as if silence could keep the break from following them to Vienna. The game first plays *closing*, then moves two years beyond the credits. Youko sends an email with news from home and a compressed file at the end. Haruki downloads and opens it. Setsuna appears on the screen holding his old guitar, announces *POWDER SNOW*, and begins to sing.[^video]

*Two years into Kazusa True, Setsuna's recorded image, Haruki's old guitar, and her song enter the room in Vienna. The frame itself does not establish forgiveness. PS3 gameplay frame: ©2012 AQUAPLUS / via xueyinhualuo, Bilibili.*

Setsuna and the friends Haruki cut off recorded the video. Haruki and Kazusa listen together, and their life in Vienna remains intact. The people left in Japan finally enter the room through a file, an old guitar, and a song. Haruki has completed his social farewells. He cannot end their shared history on everyone else's behalf.

Kazusa True carries choice farther than either earlier route. Haruki not only acknowledges desire; he personally breaks the engagement, resigns, hands over his work, and leaves the country. The price he pays is public. Yet paying his own price does not entitle him to estimate another person's capacity to endure. Filtering the facts for Setsuna is not the same as letting her answer. This route gives execution its fullest form and thereby turns informed response into the next threshold that choice must cross.

Setsuna True begins elsewhere. Haruki tells Setsuna what happened and then asks her to deal with the crisis before them. From that point on, Setsuna carries the three forward.

## Setsuna True: Setsuna Goes to Kazusa and Still Has to Bring Things to a Close

The route begins with the disclosure in Osaka. Haruki can say, "I only wanted to see you," hiding that he is also fleeing Kazusa, and then reconcile with Setsuna through sex. Or he can tell her about Kazusa. In that branch they do not kiss, embrace, or have sex. They speak about Kazusa and their own feelings. When Haruki says "I love Setsuna" at the final choice, she has already heard enough from him to change her judgment. She does not have to rely on another person's clues or expose his concealment herself. She can become angry, refuse, or decide whether to intervene.

Haruki then asks Setsuna to save Kazusa. Setsuna hears the priorities clearly: Haruki has placed Kazusa before himself and is asking her to save Kazusa, not him. She agrees that Kazusa matters most for the moment. Haruki adds that once the crisis is over, he will accept whatever Setsuna decides about their relationship. Setsuna takes on the immediate rescue; the later judgment over their relationship is something Haruki assigns her in advance.[^setsunatrue]

Setsuna lays out what life after Youko might require: doctors, funeral arrangements, relatives, work, and continued contact. Even if Youko dies, people will help Kazusa arrange the funeral, settle professional matters, and live through the days that follow. Kazusa calls such love a convenient lie. Setsuna still says that she loves her. The two then argue over whether that sentence can become visits, performances, and another meeting.

The confrontation in the snow cannot end with another farewell. Haruki is absent. Setsuna asks why Kazusa left five years ago and why she has dragged their three-person problem into the present. Kazusa answers that Setsuna already has everything and is lecturing the person who has lost it all. They call each other a coward and a hypocrite, slap and pull at each other, apologize, and attack again. The argument produces no neat answer. When it ends, they still have to face the same concert and another meeting.[^setsunatrue]

![Kazusa leans backward as Setsuna raises her hand during their bodily confrontation; Haruki is not in the frame.](./white-album-2-third-person-returns/coda-setsuna-true-direct-conflict.jpg)

*With Haruki absent, Kazusa and Setsuna confront each other through speech and bodily action. The frame does not decide a moral victor. PS3 gameplay frame: ©2012 AQUAPLUS / via xueyinhualuo, Bilibili.*

Setsuna has greater command of words and can draw on family and workplace ties. Kazusa strikes, mocks, and threatens to leave. Each interrupts the other and forces her to speak again. They do not reconcile, but they can reject each other's accounts face to face and carry what remains unsettled toward the same performance.

Kazusa later admits that she still loves Haruki, feels jealous of Setsuna, and asks not to be excluded. Setsuna does not make her withdraw the feeling. She calls Haruki and asks him to pick up the guitar; Haruki switches to speakerphone. The performance without a piano at the end of the previous essay could send its song only one way to Kazusa in the distance. Now Kazusa can hear and answer as they play. They agree that she will bring the piano next time and that the three will record a complete version, setting both a time and a part for each person. The song no longer stops at a one-way message.

Looking back on IC, Setsuna admits that she demanded the trio stay together while moving first to make Haruki part of a couple. Kazusa admits her jealousy and that she cannot treat Setsuna only as an enemy. At the end, Kazusa asks Haruki to return to Setsuna and says that he cannot continue treating the two women in the same way. Haruki names the difference: he likes Kazusa, loves Setsuna, and will marry Setsuna.

Kazusa can remain as a friend and musical collaborator. Setsuna is still the person Haruki will marry. Kazusa may dine and record with them and continue loving Haruki, but the three do not become equal romantic partners.

After proposing, Haruki presses Setsuna for an answer. He admits that his feelings for Kazusa made him waver, that he left Setsuna to resolve everything between him and Kazusa, and that he kept all of it from Setsuna. Setsuna asks whether he expects her to forgive everything in one day and save him yet again. She slaps him, then begins sending the emails she had left unsent for a long time. The notifications arrive one after another, carrying the support, jealousy, fear, anger, and resentment over professional secrecy that she had kept behind "I understand" and "I'm fine."[^mail]

Setsuna asks for five minutes and tells Haruki to forget forever whatever she says during them. She voices her jealousy, suspicion, self-disgust, and anger. In the same speech, however, she promises that after those five minutes she will understand everything and remain his place to return to. Before the interval has elapsed, the outcome is already written into the terms of her anger, and the narration hears it as a yes. Her anger gains a voice, but not time in which to change the answer.

The editors then complete the special issue. The Ogiso family invite Kazusa to dinner. The three turn lyrics, composition, guitar, piano, and vocals into a record. In the studio, Kazusa and Setsuna stand together beside the piano and equipment while Haruki remains outside the frame. A work schedule now requires the three to meet and complete something that can be released.

![In a recording studio, Setsuna and Kazusa stand together before a piano and audio equipment.](./white-album-2-third-person-returns/coda-setsuna-true-recording-studio.jpg)

*The three meet again in the recording studio, where lyrics, guitar, piano, vocals, and recording become work they do together. PS3 gameplay frame: ©2012 AQUAPLUS / via xueyinhualuo, Bilibili.*

The ending CGs for *Toki no Mahou* show the new routine. Kazusa remains openly present but outside the engagement. Setsuna accepts the proposal and will live with a man who still loves Kazusa. Haruki keeps his job, friends, and family. The route begins when Haruki asks for help. Setsuna visits Kazusa, confronts her, and calls Haruki to pick up the guitar, bringing all three back into the same song. Kazusa then pushes the final decision over marriage back to Haruki and Setsuna.

Setsuna True gives all three the most time to speak and respond. Love, jealousy, and rivalry can be voiced, while family, work, and music ensure that they will meet again. It adds two things absent from the earlier routes: Kazusa and Setsuna answer each other without Haruki speaking between them, and the three set a date for their next shared piece of work. The strain still gathers around Setsuna. She rescues Kazusa, listens to both Haruki and Kazusa, restores contact, and finally folds her own anger back into "understanding."

Setsuna's five minutes make the limit concrete. She may be angry, but first promises that after the anger she will remain Haruki's place to return to. If the person most capable of accommodating others must promise in advance that she will not leave, then direct conflict and another scheduled meeting still have not distributed the labor of repair fairly.

In all four routes, the decision changes hands midway. Haruki chooses in private in Coda Normal, and the engagement, families, and work carry his choice into daily life while the script skips public disclosure. In the Affair route, Kazusa defines the direction and deadline, Haruki makes the secret into a life, neither completes the separation alone, and Setsuna brings him back to ordinary life. Haruki initiates the public break in Kazusa True and Kazusa confirms it, while Setsuna and their friends bear the consequences in Japan. Setsuna True begins with Haruki asking for help. Setsuna rebuilds contact among the three; Kazusa pushes the choice over marriage back to Haruki and Setsuna, and Haruki proposes, but Setsuna still has to answer.

The person who speaks first does not always finish the act. The person who bears the consequences may never hear the full account. Kazusa True makes Haruki's social costs most visible, yet he filters the facts for Setsuna. Setsuna True finally gives Kazusa and Setsuna a direct conflict, while most of the repair still falls to Setsuna. Haruki punishes himself most severely in the Affair route, but the script assigns no single agent to the final separation, and Setsuna returns him to daily life. Coda Normal offers the most stable life while omitting the scene of disclosure and reply.

It is easy, then, to wish for a route that combines Haruki's public responsibility in Kazusa True with the direct conflict between Kazusa and Setsuna in Setsuna True. The position "between" the two True endings is not a fifth ending hidden in the game. It names a set of conditions that the four routes divide among themselves: the chooser states his desire and bears its public consequences; those affected hear enough of the facts before the ending closes and can refuse or demand revision; and the work of repair no longer falls to the person most willing to endure.

Coda writes each of these conditions somewhere but never gives all of them to the three at once. Its deepest branch comes after the question of whom Haruki loves: who may answer, whose answer can alter the result, and who keeps a shared life running. The four endings settle the immediate destination. None can erase the history the three already share.

[The next essay, "History Remains After the Endings"](/en/posts/white-album-2-history-after-endings/) picks up after the credits and follows the responsibilities that have changed hands: who ends a relationship, who gives an answer, and who handles the aftermath. It then asks what happens when those responsibilities enter daily life—how they are carried out, fail, and are redistributed.
