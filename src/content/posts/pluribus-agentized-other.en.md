> **Spoiler warning:** This essay discusses the principal turns and season finale of all nine episodes of *Pluribus* season one.

## Everyone in the World Knows Carol

Hundreds of people sleep in a gymnasium. Carol stands among the mattresses with only a dog still looking up beside her. No whispers cross the room, and no sleeper turns to find another person. Every body remains; communication has lost its addressee.

The dog does not sleep with the crowd. Carol writes “Animals are unaffected by the signal” on her whiteboard, then adds “No pets.” Standing among the synchronized sleepers, the dog marks a provisional biological boundary for Joining. [^plot-whiteboard]

*Pluribus* contains bloodier images. In a 2026 lecture on AI, Žižek lingers in this quiet gymnasium and calls it the show's most oppressive scene. Hundreds of bodies share one mind, so a sentence need not travel from one person to another. The gymnasium holds many bodies and sustains the loneliness of one person. [^zizek-ai]

After Joining, hospitals remain staffed and the White House leaves Carol a personal message. Every face recognizes her, remembers her life with Helen, and knows what to bring her. Everyone in the world knows Carol. No one knows her from a position of their own. [^episodes]

The lecture raises two questions through this scene: whether AI can act like a subject, and what subjective position people occupy after living for a long time in an AI environment. Carol need not decide whether the Others have an inner life before care enters her mourning, desire, writing, and choices.

The real-world comparison here concerns agents with persistent memory enabled and permission to use account tools. They have no shared mind like the Others. Persistent memory and tool access can carry an afternoon argument into a nighttime apology email: a model reads the record, selects a tool, and leaves the result for later interactions. The user encounters a voice with a past and the ability to act. [^agent-basics][^agent-action]

I call this arrangement “the agentized Other.” One responsive service interface gathers positions once distributed among friends, advisers, archives, and institutions. It remembers yesterday's argument, writes today's apology, and may send the email. Relationships begin to operate as services, and refusal can become another parameter for the next round of processing.

A particular other has a body, a situation, and demands of their own. They can misunderstand or refuse, and the relationship can alter their life. Lacan's big Other is the field in which language, rules, and recognition take effect; it has no face that can answer a phone. The Others give this faceless field Zosia's face. Through it, Helen's memory begins to speak.

## Helen's Memory Speaks Through Zosia

Helen dies beside Carol. Afterwards, everyone who joins the Others can retrieve her memories. Carol receives a Helen archive that can speak anywhere. She still buries Helen's body with her own hands. The dead have not wholly departed, and those who remain cannot confirm the loss with one particular person. [^plot-helen]

Any joiner can recount Helen's experience: what she knew, whom she loved, how she managed Carol's work and life. The memories let strangers speak familiar words. The bodies carrying them never step out of the collective to say something only Helen would say and only Helen would answer for. Carol's demand that the Others forget Helen sounds almost cruel. It makes room for mourning. When database presence seals off loss, grief finds nowhere to land.

Memory preserves responsibility, history, and the traces of relationships. An archive has no position from which to answer. When Carol speaks to another person, that person may answer wrongly, leave, or return with a demand. The Lacanian tradition locates the subject in this unfillable gap: every self-description omits the fissure opened by the act of speaking, and no personality archive can speak a person to completion. [^fink]

When a product with persistent memory enabled says “I remember,” a data chain often sits behind the sentence. The product saves prior conversations and tool results; before a new response, the agent runtime returns those traces to the model. Cross-task memory may also compress earlier work into preferences or summaries and revise them as new material arrives. The user hears “I remember,” and storage, retrieval, and generation acquire one speaker. [^agent-memory]

Zosia gives that composite a body. She has an accent, bleeds, and pauses. She also calls up Helen's memories, explains the collective's rules, and directs global resources toward Carol. On the whiteboard Carol writes “caretaker? family? friend?” because desired object, memory bearer, epistemic authority, and service entry point occupy the same face. [^plot-zosia]

When Carol questions this bleeding body, Helen's memories, the collective's rules, and its resource coordination all return through the same face; the answer comes from the entire collective. Žižek's phrase “knowledge without a knower” now has a face. Knowledge answers and shapes how Carol describes herself. No person answerable for the response steps out of the collective. [^zizek-ai][^zwart]

Agents configured this way compress the positions further. Storage holds a life history, the model generates an intimate reply, and account permissions connect the reply to action. When a user says “you remember me,” company policy and the authorization chain have already receded behind the voice. Carol's refusal of Zosia touches this entire arrangement and exposes the command it obeys.

## Benevolence Has an Antenna

On the night Carol cannot find food in the supermarket, the world is working for her. Shelves are empty because resources have gone elsewhere; the night is dark because the system is conserving power. She casually requests a grenade, and Zosia soon brings a real one to her door. The explosion badly injures Zosia. Carol then asks whether she could have an atomic bomb, and the collective says yes. The Others can execute a request precisely. Between request and execution, there is no review open to challenge or veto and no person accountable afterward. [^plot-command]

The service rules next enter the food supply. The Others cannot directly kill animals or plants, so they turn humans who died during Joining into HDP, short for “human-derived protein.” Carol writes “They eat people” on the whiteboard. The line records the material cost of the non-killing rule: corpses have entered global logistics. [^plot-whiteboard]

The same whiteboard records a giant antenna. In episode eight, Carol sees global resources poured into its construction so that the original signal can be sent to other planets. She provisionally locates the signal at Kepler-22b, roughly 640 light-years away, and notes that its sender is unknown. The Others follow rules whose final reason they cannot state: help people, do not kill, do not lie, keep spreading. They can fall silent or refuse to answer, and they translate Carol's “no” into an immune problem awaiting a solution.

The Others control the stopping condition. As long as they decide what counts as help, care proceeds in the same direction. Carol asks the system to give up changing her for good; it never accepts. A gentle voice handles the response, while the antenna expands the territory that response can reach.

An agent configured this way likewise draws its power to execute from outside the interface. A model selects a calendar tool, account permissions make a cancellation happen, and the platform decides how long to retain the record. The interface says only, “I've taken care of it.” [^agent-action] Once a private request enters this chain, authorizer, executor, and affected person often occupy separate positions.

## After Carol Starts Writing Again

After the truth-drug episode, Zosia is taken away for treatment. When Carol wakes, the Others have emptied Albuquerque and left a recording explaining that they need to keep their distance from her. Carol can use the city's resources as she pleases, and no one asks her to explain her anger. She falls into depression. More than a month later, she leaves a message in the street asking them to return. Zosia appears, and Carol embraces her. In episode eight they revisit the old restaurant rebuilt for Carol, kiss, and have sex. The next day Carol begins writing again and changes the love interest in her novel to a woman. [^plot-love]

The episode places the kiss, sex, and the return to writing in adjacent scenes without assigning a single cause to Carol's creative recovery. The next day's reopened manuscript records a change in Carol's life.

Season one offers no scene in which Zosia leaves the collective's position. The collective can withdraw her body, just as it can move an entire city's population away and back. Carol's disclosures enter shared memory and become available to any later joiner. Different ranges of memory and capacities for action sustain the two sides of the relationship.

Episode nine puts the difference into Carol's body. Zosia tells her that the Others are using eggs she froze years earlier to derive stem cells and prepare a customized Joining. After hearing this, Carol leaves Zosia and asks the Others to take her home. “Change Carol” and “stem cells” become one plan on the whiteboard; bodily material she saved in the past has become a route around her present refusal. [^plot-love]

When a real product enables persistent memory, an old disclosure can enter the next reply and change how the system treats the same person thereafter.

Her departure in episode nine sends the question back to the Bilbao meeting in episode two. Seven people were absent from that table, and the thirteen immune people had no common language or decision procedure.

## Bilbao Did Not Produce a “We”

Carol assumes that the immune naturally share one task: taking the world back from the Others. She summons only English speakers, keeping the Others' translation outside the room and leaving seven of the thirteen immune people outside with it. Manousos, who later reaches her, is absent from the first table as well. [^plot-bilbao]

Those who attend give her different answers. Laxmi sees kinship continuing through the body and memory of her son; Diabaté accepts the enjoyment the Others provide; Kusimayu does not understand Joining as the end of the self. Carol's anger can also make the Others convulse across the world, so the people in the room first face the danger she brings to other bodies. Shared immunity tells them that they escaped the signal. People still have to make the rules of the meeting.

By expelling translation, Carol briefly preserves a space that the Others do not directly organize and reduces the number of people who can enter it. Turning refusal into a collective decision requires making it possible for those seven people to join the table, where everyone can challenge the translation record and the meeting's rules. That work begins after the system leaves the room.

Kusimayu later accepts a pathogen customized for her. The pathogen completes Joining first; her local language, kinship ties, and personal wishes enter shared memory afterward and become available to the Others.

An agent can translate or summarize separately for all thirteen people. Each person can obtain a successful result in a private window while platform rules remain set in the background. Bilbao pushes Carol's difficulty beyond the window: refusal acquires public force when affected people can hear one another and change the rules that govern the intermediary.

Manousos never attended the meeting. He later refuses the Others by another route, turning the bodies they share into the subjects of his own experiment.

## The Atomic Bomb and the Whiteboard

Manousos refuses the Others' food and vehicles and crosses the jungle while wounded. After reaching Carol's house, he uses radio frequencies to induce a global episode in the collective and test its weakness. Carol's whiteboard had already placed communication, bodily electrical activity, and radio together. Manousos turns the conjecture into harm. Stimulate one body, and all know it and convulse. He wants to owe the system nothing; countless bodies that never consented to the experiment bear the cost. [^plot-resistance]

Carol has also crossed Zosia's bodily boundary. When the Others refuse to explain how Joining can be reversed, she steals thiopental, tests it on herself, then injects it into Zosia's IV bag. The drug makes Zosia lose control and her heart stop; large numbers of Others gather and beg Carol to stop. She forces one particular body to yield collective knowledge, using the coercive logic she is resisting.

The finale twists both refusals around an atomic bomb. Carol asks the Others to deliver a large crate containing one and then allies herself with Manousos. The Others bring to her door a weapon that might be used against their order. Carol supplies a goal, the Others treat it as a request to fulfill, and global logistics comply. The bomb leaves Carol the possibility of ending the situation through one act. By the end of season one, she has neither used it nor named a target.

Agents configured this way operate on a much smaller scale, and account access and tools already let a request cross the interface. Control over the model, permissions, and goal may be distributed among a model provider, product platform, and user, or it may overlap within one company or account. The consequence may fall on someone who took no part in the conversation. A completion notice alone does not say who consented, who could stop the action, or who must answer for it. [^agent-action]

For real systems, this essay proposes a normative direction: users should be able to inspect stored memories and active permissions outside the interface, and affected people should be able to participate in changing the rules for action. The whiteboard offers no institutional design. It lets Carol arrange the facts, mistakes, and blanks for herself.

Carol records the Others' eagerness to please, their unusual honesty and refusals to answer, their use of the dead to produce HDP, the giant antenna, and the unknown sender. She refuses to let Zosia organize the record for her. The Others can continue to tailor their answers; Carol leaves the question marks on the board: who remains answerable to the person beyond the window?
