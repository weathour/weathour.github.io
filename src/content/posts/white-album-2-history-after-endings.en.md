The chain on the door was still unlatched.

After the Affair / Kazusa Normal route, Setsuna continued to enter Haruki's apartment with her own key. Haruki was on leave from work and visited a psychosomatic medicine clinic once a week. He told Setsuna to stop coming and leave him alone, then admitted that he was lying. If she stopped appearing altogether, he would not know what to do.

Haruki pointed out the contradiction himself. He could have fastened the chain or taken back the key if he had meant to keep her out. He had done neither. His words sent her away while his actions kept the entrance open.

[The previous essay, "The Third Person Returns Through Work"](/en/posts/white-album-2-third-person-returns/) followed Coda's decisions as they changed hands. In Coda Normal, Haruki makes an inward choice; engagement, family, and work carry it into daily life, while disclosure remains unwritten. In the Affair route, Kazusa defines the form and deadline, and Haruki makes the secret into a life. Kazusa announces the endpoint; neither partner will bear the final separation alone, and the narration does not say who pulls away first. Setsuna inherits the work of bringing Haruki back. Haruki initiates the public rupture of Kazusa True, and Kazusa forces him to confirm it. Setsuna True begins with Haruki asking for help; Setsuna rebuilds the relationship, Kazusa withdraws her equal romantic claim, Haruki proposes marriage, and Setsuna still gives the final answer. The endings settle each relationship for the moment. Daily life does not end with them.

The three additional stories give three of those endings different futures. *不倶戴天の君へ* (working translation: *To You, My Sworn Enemy*) follows the Affair route, also called Kazusa Normal. *幸せへと戻る道* (*The Road Back to Happiness*) follows Kazusa True, while *幸せへと進む道* (*The Road Toward Happiness*) follows Setsuna True. Coda Normal already reaches a point one year later in the game, but it received no independent after story of comparable scale. The three stories belong to mutually exclusive timelines. A quarrel, homecoming, wedding, or pregnancy in one route cannot fill the blank left by another.[^versions]

The stories keep testing that division of labor. Work goes wrong; people who left Japan return to the old house; Setsuna, having taken charge of repair, can no longer sustain the care it requires. What happens next depends on who speaks first, who acts, and who can answer or alter the arrangement already in place.

*This essay contains full spoilers for Coda's four endings and for 不倶戴天の君へ, 幸せへと戻る道, and 幸せへと進む道. The cover is an original conceptual image. Four gameplay frames from the additional stories appear solely for noncommercial criticism; copyright remains with AQUAPLUS, and the source footage was uploaded to Bilibili by 次郎JIRO and 糖炒小熊clover.*[^source]

## The Door Chain Remains Unlatched, and Staying Put Is Already a Choice

On an April weekend, Setsuna does Haruki's laundry, cooks for him, and holds him as usual. Haruki admits that the care satisfies him. Setsuna admits that being needed satisfies her too. These acts keep him fed and his apartment running. They also spare both of them from reopening the relationship for the moment: one waits for the door to open, while the other inhabits the role of the only person who can save him.

After spending the night, Setsuna decides that her presence has made Haruki's condition worse and leaves while he is asleep. She later tells someone at work that the two of them had a terrible fight. Haruki's breakdown, leave of absence, and treatment are compressed for the outside world into an ordinary quarrel between lovers.

Haruki then asks Takeya what to do about work, Setsuna, and the rest of his life. He says that he has never decided anything and has only drifted to this point. Takeya refuses to choose for him. Haruki knew other paths existed and remained where he was; Takeya tells him that this, too, was a decision.[^door]

That answer exposes the way Haruki has described the relationship. He has begun going out in stages as his doctor advised, and he has resumed written contact with Setsuna. She still has the key and continues to insist on visiting. His body's limited capacity does not explain whether he wants her to stay or leave. Setsuna has not remained still either. She discusses his return to work and her visits in writing, though she still cannot bring herself to call him. Her language shifts from wanting to support Haruki to having to support him, and she lies to her family, saying that the two of them have always loved each other without trouble.

In IC, Haruki promised never to cut Setsuna off unless she asked him to. CC had already shown how he could keep the words of that promise while withdrawing from conversation, kisses, and the ordinary life of lovers for three years. *To You, My Sworn Enemy* places the same practice at a door. Haruki neither retrieves the key nor ends the relationship in words. He asks Setsuna to stop visiting and leaves the act of departure to the person most afraid of being abandoned.

Setsuna's body gives out before the language of duty does.

On the way to one visit, she begins sweating heavily at the station. Her legs lock, and nausea keeps her from moving. When she finally reaches Haruki's apartment, she blames the delay on work and forces herself through the familiar routine of chores, conversation, and care. She wants to look after him. She also fears that stopping will cost her place in the relationship. Several desires carry her toward the door while her body refuses to continue.

Haruki's later demand that she stay away differs from the one at the opening. His own illness has taught him to recognize that she is collapsing too. He wants to keep her from following him down, and he is still waiting for her to resist what he says. Both wishes can be true. The scene offers no purer private intention hidden beneath them.

Setsuna returns to work but remains unable to reach Haruki's apartment on weekends. A television preview then announces Kazusa's Paris performance. The broadcast shows a pianist advancing through her European career. Outside that frame, Kazusa is dealing with Youko's illness, the pressure of the performance, and years of isolation while waiting for someone to reassure her. Television crosses the national border with ease and separates public success from private distress.

When neither Haruki nor Setsuna can move their relationship further, Kazusa is the one who makes the next call.

## Once the Call Connects, Why Speaking Plainly Is Still Not Enough

The previous essay discussed the conflict between Kazusa and Setsuna in Setsuna True. Setsuna has heard facts from Haruki that can change her judgment. At his request, she goes to Kazusa and then directs the rescue and the rebuilding that follow. The telephone call in *To You, My Sworn Enemy* belongs to another history. Kazusa has announced the end of their winter together. Their hands part, but the narration does not identify who pulls away first. She then entrusts Haruki's rehabilitation to Setsuna, who is the first to admit that she cannot carry it.

Setsuna prepares to contact the European agency. Kazusa calls her instead, dialing from memory after deleting the number from her phone.

They begin with polite conversation about performances, work, and Takahiro. They avoid Haruki and their own distress. Setsuna gives a false account of her life. Kazusa hides Youko's illness, the crisis surrounding her performance, and her isolation. They stay with safe subjects so that the call will not end too soon.

Kazusa says good night and almost ends the call. Then she stops Setsuna and gets only as far as "I..." Setsuna names the failure first. She apologizes, admits that she may no longer be able to save "our person," and says that she has failed the task Kazusa once entrusted to her. Kazusa asks how matters reached this point. Her shock turns into accusation only afterward. Setsuna does not explicitly cry "help me" until that attack is already under way.[^call]

Setsuna first places her failure before Kazusa. After pressing her for an explanation, Kazusa invokes the way she once cried and withdrew, then begins attacking her. Setsuna only then says outright that she wants Kazusa's help. Kazusa must face another fact as well. Entrusting Haruki to the person she trusted and envied most did not end her own responsibility. That night, neither woman can keep citing that earlier handoff as proof that she has already done her part.

The quarrel soon turns ugly. Kazusa demands that Setsuna give Haruki back. Setsuna refuses. She is willing to help Kazusa in other matters, even to go to Paris or Vienna to care for Youko, but she will not "return" Haruki. Words such as "take back" and "hand over" still turn him into someone the women can transfer between them. Setsuna also makes her own condition clear: she wants Kazusa to share the burden that has crushed her, but she does not intend to leave Haruki.

The narration does not polish this direct speech into a victory for honesty. The women insult each other, sob, pretend not to hear, defend themselves, and push responsibility onto the other. They can say what they want and still use "for Haruki's sake" as a weapon against each other.

Kazusa finally says that Haruki must decide. The sentence cuts across the idea that the women can transfer him between them. Setsuna then says that she will not stop acting even if Haruki abandons her one day. His choice cannot determine the course of the other two women's love, hostility, or lives for them. Kazusa and Setsuna claim the decision as "our decision," then part as eternal enemies who love each other intensely. Hostility does not consume the love, and love does not reduce the hostility to a misunderstanding.

The next day, Setsuna says that she is hungry. She starts cooking, packs the food into a lunchbox, and prepares to eat it with Haruki. She tells her mother that she spent the night fighting with a friend on the phone, then states openly that she still loves Haruki as he was, as he is, and as he will be. She resumes her visits and is prepared to move out if her family continues to stand in their way. Returning to Haruki is now a desire she acknowledges, not only a duty that "no one else can perform."

After the call, Setsuna is the first to act again: she leaves home. Haruki continues to recover through medication, treatment, time, and help from the people around him. Only after Setsuna comes through the door does he admit that his notes have been waiting for her.[^aftermath]

Neither of them calls the day a cure. Setsuna is prepared to spend her life on what follows, and Haruki knows that his symptoms may return. They have only begun to act again.

Later, Haruki and Setsuna watch Kazusa's Paris performance and interview together. On the public screen, Kazusa leaves them with "Serves you right." Haruki is certain only that the remark is not addressed to him alone. Setsuna hears a declaration that the two women will remain enemies forever. Haruki hears a farewell carrying love within it. Each reading belongs to a character; the script does not select a single recipient for the audience. Television brings Kazusa into the room, where Haruki and Setsuna can listen but cannot answer her.

*To You, My Sworn Enemy* does not repeat the argument in Setsuna True. In Setsuna True, Setsuna has heard enough to answer, takes the initiative to go to Kazusa, and arranges the performance, the telephone call, and another meeting after their conflict. This call reopens the repair work that the Affair route left to Setsuna. Kazusa regains the power to confront her; after admitting failure, Setsuna chooses once more to return to Haruki. Yet the quarrel keeps circling back to who should "have" him, and it produces no future shared by all three. Before the call, Haruki describes his refusal to move as drifting. Only after Setsuna returns does he admit that he wanted her to come back.

One conflict can break a stalemate, but it cannot arrange life three years later. The after stories continue by showing Haruki evading decisions through self-punishment, deciding for other people in the name of taking care of everything, and the married couple explaining a promise broken by work. Housing, wages, medical records, relatives, and friends now enter the relationship.

## Choices Enter Daily Life: Homes, Family, Secrets, and Broken Promises

Both True after stories open inside relationships that already exist. Kazusa True ends with a rupture that Haruki initiates and owns in public. Setsuna True places most of the rebuilding and closure in Setsuna's hands. By *The Road Back to Happiness*, Haruki and Kazusa have lived together in Vienna for about three years. In *The Road Toward Happiness*, Haruki and Setsuna are married and both work in Tokyo. Neither story gives them another route button. The old division of labor now enters housework, housing, business travel, and overtime.

### Homes and Families Make the Choice Habitable

Haruki initiates the public rupture in Kazusa True; after Kazusa forces him to confirm it aloud, the two move to Vienna. By the after story, he is her husband, manager, agency employee, and daily caregiver. Kazusa remains connected to the outside world through her performances, the agency, Youko, and Miyoko. Haruki plans her schedule, manages the money, and attends to her daily life. He has taken on almost all of it.

Three years of marriage have not dissolved Kazusa's fear of being left. During their first night back in Japan, the two still bring up divorce in an argument. After they sleep in separate rooms for a short time, Kazusa fears that Haruki may truly abandon her. She depends on the same person as husband, manager, and caregiver.

Continued treatment has left Youko's condition stable for the time being. She draws on the agency and her personal connections to restore the Touma house, negotiate with the school, and visit another family again and again. On the fifth visit, the family finally lets her through the door. "That family's daughter" then begins helping her persuade them; the script never gives the daughter a name.[^kazusaafter]

The old house is restored to match their memories, though important objects do not return to their former places. Kazusa's original piano remains in Vienna, and the Japanese house receives a new one. Haruki picks up a guitar, then replaces it on the stand when Kazusa enters. He believes that she understands why he no longer plays in front of her. The couple can return to the house and school. The IC relationship formed by piano, guitar, and voice does not reappear with the setting.

Youko and Haruki arrange a New Year's Eve wedding in Houjou University Attached High School's Second Music Room, and Haruki prepares a ring. By the time Kazusa puts on the dress, she and Haruki have been legally married for years and have lived together for about three. She protests against the surprise, then states that she will answer the vow. Only then does Haruki tell the friends waiting outside. Kazusa answers for herself, though Youko and Haruki have completed most of the preparations for the house, the school, and the wedding.

Youko keeps the old house as another place to belong. The couple can return after a concert, for a holiday, to see friends, or whenever they miss home. They do not settle in Japan again, but they now have a home to which they can return.

When Kazusa goes out on the streets of Japan, she first puts on glasses and a hat and uses the alias Kazumi Higashino. She later kisses Haruki in the street. They no longer have to hide inside a hotel room, but they have not made themselves known to everyone either.

Setsuna True places most of its rebuilding and closure in Setsuna's hands. The after story begins by splitting daily life between the spouses. Haruki and Setsuna both work, save money, and prepare to move into a 2LDK apartment. Haruki takes charge of laundry and cleaning. Setsuna treats cooking as the last shred of dignity left to her as a wife. They keep negotiating housework against their working hours and their ideas of themselves. The Ogiso family helps with the move, and Setsuna's mother has begun attending plays and sharing meals with Haruki's mother. Family contact has resumed, though the story still does not show Haruki facing his mother.

Help from the Ogisos still makes Haruki uneasy. From his perspective, the family barges into entanglements he has always avoided and goes about things noisily and inefficiently. He has begun to trust that even when they scold him, they will not expel him from the family.

Takeya and Io remember the three years Setsuna missed, and Setsuna can now ask them to finish telling those stories. Their later disagreement about income, promotion, and parental leave concerns their own future. It is not a childcare plan Haruki and Setsuna have already adopted. These friends can discuss old injuries and also carry unresolved conflicts between work and care into the room.

In Kazusa's route, Haruki and Kazusa can return only after Youko restores the old house, contacts the school, and knocks repeatedly at another family's door. They also gain a few voices willing to witness the wedding. Kazusa chooses to return to Japan and, after protesting, answers the vow herself; her answer comes after many of the arrangements have already been made. In Setsuna's route, the marriage rests on work shared by the spouses and renewed contact between their families. Every home still has an inside and an outside. Some people can enter and help; others arrive only as voices at a wedding or a letter in the post.

### Well-Meant Secrets Can Still Decide for Someone Else

Time has not removed lies and concealment from either marriage.

Kazusa does not want to discuss Youko's illness in detail, while Haruki insists on knowing her condition. He privately plans Kazusa's life after Youko dies, including when Kazusa would have a child and who will care for her afterward. He also assumes that Youko understands he is preparing for that handover. Youko has discussed her treatment in Kazusa's presence, and the script never says that she and Haruki falsify a diagnosis. Kazusa has given no answer to Haruki's plan.

The surprise wedding is also kept from Kazusa. The marriage already exists; the surprise changes its ceremony. Kazusa objects first, then states that she will answer the vow. Only then does Haruki contact the friends waiting outside. She can still protest and answer in her own voice. When Haruki privately plans the timing of having a child and the handover of care after Youko's death, the script gives Kazusa no such answer.

Setsuna's route has its secrets too. Her mother has already begun spending time with Haruki's mother. Setsuna says that she knows nothing about it, while Haruki reads her mischievous expression as evidence that she has known all along. Haruki also keeps matters he believes will not alter their life at present to himself.

Whether a secret removes the other person's power to decide depends on what remains possible after it surfaces: can that person answer and revise the plan? A broken Christmas Eve promise makes the distinction concrete.

### After a Promise Breaks, How Do Two People Meet Again?

Kazusa's after story offers another kind of promise. Before the wedding formally begins, Haruki promises once more to stay with her forever. Kazusa replies that she has made the same vow a million times already and can make it for the million-and-first time. The delayed ceremony is about to bring their established marriage before friends, and they confirm it again in the Second Music Room. The story stops when others witness the vow; it does not show the promise failing later.

Setsuna's after story lets a promise fail once. Haruki and Setsuna solemnly agree that both will be home before ten on Christmas Eve. They know how much history such a holiday promise carries. Then a cabinet minister's resignation produces breaking news for Haruki. A defect in a product released by Setsuna's company the day before triggers a full recall, and she is sent to apologize at shops across Tokyo. Work holds both of them away from home, and both break the promise.

On the midnight phone call, Haruki apologizes and explains how much work remains. Only then does Setsuna tell him that she has failed to get home too. She has not simply been waiting for him alone, and her own emergency has made her abandon the morning demand that he hand even an exclusive story to his superior. They agree to meet after work, with Haruki to call again when he finishes.

Setsuna eventually waits outside Haruki's office. They talk again about the considerate lies and absolute vows of their past. They still say that they will keep the next promise, knowing that they may fail again. This time they explain what happened, set another time to meet, and meet outside his office.[^setsunaafter]

Both take part in the repair, though the burden is uneven. Setsuna travels to Haruki's office and does most of the waiting. After they meet, the two go to a nearby hotel they have already booked and spend the night awake. The repair work that Setsuna largely carried in Coda is only beginning to be shared.

In *To You, My Sworn Enemy*, Haruki describes his refusal to choose as drifting, while Setsuna describes her visits as a necessity. The two sustain themselves through signals and sheer bodily effort. The promise in the Setsuna True after story is no weaker, but failure need not lead at once to self-punishment or silence. Kazusa's after story leaves its vow at the moment of delayed witness; Setsuna's after story shows how two people continue after breaking one. These are different marriages, and a scene from one cannot finish the work left undone by the other.

None of the three stories brings its characters to the point of leaving a relationship. Kazusa can object to the wedding, but the room is already prepared and the guests are waiting outside. Haruki and Setsuna can reschedule a meeting; neither couple reaches a decision to separate. The existing texts leave open who can depart and how care or contact would be arranged afterward.

In both True after stories, the person outside the marriage returns only in indirect form: as anonymous figures and voices in one route, and as a postcard in the other.

### How the Third Person Returns

The Kazusa True after story offers only a few anonymous figures. An unnamed woman visited six months earlier. Kazusa says that she once loved, admired, and betrayed "her," and that "she" has forgiven her. When Youko visits the other family, "that family's daughter" helps her. Readers may readily take both figures to be Setsuna, but the script never lets either one speak under that name.

The wedding closes with the voices of "Friend 1," "Friend 2," and "Friend 3." A player may readily think of Takeya, Io, and Setsuna. The text confirms none of those names and gives no basis for assigning each numbered voice to one person. Every claim that forgiveness has been granted comes through Haruki's, Kazusa's, or Youko's judgment and report. The person who was harmed never says under her own name what she has forgiven. Kazusa also admits that even if "she" has forgiven her, she still cannot approach that person or forgive herself.

Setsuna True gives Kazusa a different place. As Haruki and Setsuna leave their old apartment, a neighbor hands them a postcard addressed to the adjacent unit and therefore delivered next door. Setsuna reads that Kazusa's Vienna performance succeeded and that she will return to Japan after New Year. Haruki guesses that Kazusa knew the correct apartment number, wrote the adjacent one on purpose, and timed the card for moving day. The intention remains his conjecture. The couple can read her news together without hiding the card.

The couple can now discuss Kazusa openly. She will return to Japan after New Year, but she does not belong to the daily life of their new home.

Haruki fears becoming like his father. Even without confidence that he can be a good father, he says that he wants a child. Setsuna then tells him that the test was positive and that the hospital confirmed the pregnancy the day before. Haruki states his wish before Setsuna gives him the result; an established fact has not forced the answer from him.

The third person named at the ending is the child. The child turns the couple into a family of three without inheriting Kazusa's place in the old triangle. Haruki and Setsuna still remember Kazusa together; the child will live in the new home as someone they care for together.

## One Blank in the Text, Three Histories That Cannot Be Combined

Read side by side, the routes tempt players to assemble another history from their parts: take Haruki's public breakup and acceptance of its consequences from Kazusa True; take Setsuna's decision to seek out Kazusa, their conflict, and her arrangements for another meeting from Setsuna True; then take Kazusa's power to confront Setsuna in turn from *To You, My Sworn Enemy* and add the reunion after a promise both spouses have broken. The pieces seem to make a more complete route.

Players have read all three routes. Each character has lived through only one. Anything they learn later bears the history they cannot erase.

The Affair route leaves repair to Setsuna. *To You, My Sworn Enemy* reopens the repair work that Kazusa entrusted to Setsuna, while Haruki waits until Setsuna returns before admitting that he needs her. Kazusa's after story continues the marriage produced by Haruki's public rupture, but Youko, the agency, and the school must prepare the couple's return. The text leaves the response from those in Japan uncertain: it gives us only reports, anonymous figures, and voices. Setsuna's after story begins to divide one act of repair between the spouses, though Setsuna still does most of the traveling and waiting, while Kazusa remains outside the home.

Coda Normal gives us only the in-game life one year later; the official text catalog checked for this essay contains no independent after story of comparable scale. Haruki and Setsuna are preparing for a wedding six months away and tell each other "I love you" every day. His feeling for Kazusa, which he believes may last for life, remains in his internal monologue. The text stops there. It never tells us whether the feeling later enters their conversation.

The previous essay's position "between the two True endings" names a set of conditions that have never appeared together; it does not require putting all three people in the same room. Those affected must hear enough of the facts to judge before the arrangement closes. Their answers must be able to alter the result and cannot be written for them by someone else. Haruki would have to state his own decision. Kazusa could make demands of Setsuna and be angry with her; Setsuna could refuse without always becoming the person who cleans up last. No one could make private arrangements for the other two about medical care, housing, or who looks after whom. They might build another relationship afterward, or they might separate.

Even if they separate, each of them should decide after hearing the facts that could change their judgment. Silence, anonymous reports, and a single caregiver can no longer close the matter for everyone. The three after stories each reach some of these conditions, but none gives all of them to the three at once.

The first essay showed how three relationships jointly formed the triangle. The second traced how Kazusa's absence continued to organize it, while the three heroines of CC reflected Haruki from different positions. The third asked who acts on desire in each route, whether those affected can know and answer, and who inherits the work of repair. The fourth follows those handoffs through time, asking how people carry the responsibilities, where the arrangements break down, and who takes them up next. [The next volume opens with *The Sinner's Guilt, Other People's Lives*](/en/posts/white-album-2-guilt-and-the-days-after/), turning to Japanese literature to see how these conditions emerge—and fail—within similar triangular structures.

The chain on the door remains unlatched. The old house has been repaired. The two people who broke their promise meet outside the office, and Kazusa's postcard reaches the apartment next door. The ending has passed, but the past remains present in their daily lives.
