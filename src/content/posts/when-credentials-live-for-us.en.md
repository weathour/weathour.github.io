## The Notice Made Fan Jin a “Master” Before He Did

Fan Jin stands in the market holding a chicken. His family has run out of food, and his mother has sent him to sell the bird for rice. The men carrying the examination results enter the village while he is away. His neighbors search for him and finally drag home a man who still refuses to believe the news. The notice already hangs in the house. Fan Jin sees his name, decades of examinations come due on a single sheet of paper, and his body gives way. By the time Butcher Hu slaps him out of his delirium, everyone around him has begun to observe a different etiquette.[^scholars-fan]

Butcher Hu once treated his son-in-law as a penniless pedant. Now he hardly dares touch this incarnation of the Literary Star. He straightens Fan Jin’s clothes and follows with his head bowed. Zhang Jingzhai claims kinship through an old teacher-pupil connection, calls Fan Jin a brother, and gives him money and a house. Forms of address change alongside kinship and access to resources. Fan Jin has yet to write another essay or perform a single official duty. The notice has already assigned actions to everyone else.

The money, the house and the butcher’s fear alter the relationships Fan Jin can enter and the resources he can command. These actions give the credential its hardest substance. Many people change their conduct in response to the notice, and their conduct changes the named man’s circumstances. The notice does not need Fan Jin to believe that he has become someone else. The community believes on his behalf.

Even before the notice arrives, the examination has given knowledge its shape. Mr Ma Er surveys the historical routes open to learned men: selection by speech and conduct in the Spring and Autumn period, political persuasion in the Warring States, recommendation of the worthy and upright under the Han, poetry and prose under the Tang, and Neo-Confucian learning under the Song. He ends with the present dynasty’s selection by examination essays. If the sages lived today, he says, they too would have to write the prescribed form before entering office. His account has an almost guileless honesty. After years of failure in the examinations, Ma Er does earn a living by reading scripts and editing model essays for publishers. Yet his judgment delivers knowledge back to the same exit. Scholars write in the form the institution knows how to recognize.[^scholars-ma]

Exam training establishes a durable relation. Someone else poses the question, an authorized judge assesses the answer, the result enters a ranked record, and the prize lies in a future social role. Mathematical proofs, laboratory problems and long essays can all contain genuine knowledge and intelligence. The direction of training still depends on who defines the difficulty and to whom failure must answer. When this circuit occupies the main hours of study and the principal channel of success and failure, several questions earn no points: Why this problem? Whose circumstances would the solution change? Who answers if the problem itself is wrong? Unless a course gives those questions room of their own, the authority to ask remains outside the examination hall. Students spend their strongest initiative adapting to the problem already set.

Rankings turn that adaptation into differences between people. One mistake belongs to a moment, but a series of records can rewrite it as something “this person is bad at.” A high score can expand just as easily into a general judgment about discipline, intelligence and prospects. Schools, families and employers save the cost of learning who someone is afresh. The people being judged learn to describe themselves through the same record. Even doubt and redirection have to be explained against the curve of their scores.

In *Discipline and Punish*, Michel Foucault places the examination at the intersection of hierarchical observation and normalizing judgment. Once teaching and record-keeping make examination continuous, the teacher does more than test whether a student can solve one problem at one moment. Names, marks, comments and ranks turn a person into a “case” that can be described, compared and retrieved.[^foucault-exam] This helps explain how Fan Jin’s notice travels beyond the examination hall. The scripts have been collected, but the record of judgment can still move into marriage, employment and ordinary association, telling strangers how to treat its bearer.

Standardization widens that record’s circulation. Ronald Dore’s account of the “diploma disease” identifies two joined institutional conditions. Standardized examinations give certificates a common currency. Career bureaucracies then use general educational qualifications to screen for broad ability and long-term trainability. Dore distinguishes credentials tied to training for a particular occupation from general educational certificates that organizations use to select future members. In this chain, the latter carries more than a claim about what someone can already do. It also says which occupational route the person is worth placing on.[^dore]

A credential can therefore operate like credit secured against future performance. Zhang Jingzhai gives Fan Jin a house before he takes office, investing in the power and connections a successful candidate may later acquire. Modern organizations likewise need not wait for every applicant to perform the job before screening new hires. A certificate compresses risk in advance. This lowers the cost of selection at scale and gives one educational record a social lifespan far longer than the examination itself.

Between 2012 and 2025, China’s gross enrollment rate in higher education rose from 30 percent to 61.3 percent.[^edu-scale] Those figures establish institutional scale: credential education has become a mass, routine passage. They do not tell us how often it produces the kind of subject described here. Once organizations preserve and repeatedly call upon a judgment, however, the record crosses the boundary of the examination and takes possession of a name. The notice first answers “Who is Fan Jin?” It then promises the life toward which that name will lead. A past result acquires the tense of the future.

## A Position Sent Back from the Future

Fang Hongjian spends several years studying in Europe without completing a degree he can show for them. As his return approaches, he needs a visible result to present to his father and his late fiancée’s family. He buys a doctorate from the fictitious Carleton University. The paper adds nothing to his research, but it sends the role of “foreign-trained PhD” home ahead of him. His elders can exhibit a return on their educational investment, and local society receives a ready-made name for him.[^fortress]

Fang knows the degree is fraudulent. He even maintains a private rule against listing it formally when applying for work. His distance from the credential does not undo the photograph, the newspaper report or the conduct of relatives and friends. Once other people possess the title, its social effects no longer remain under the holder’s control. Fang’s shame shows that he does not fully coincide with the role. Those around him can still use the degree to picture his respectable career and marriage prospects ahead of time.

*The Castle* carries the same structure from credential to appointment. K arrives in the village late at night and, when threatened with expulsion, says that the Count has summoned him to serve as land surveyor. A telephone call first denies that the village has any such surveyor, then recognizes the earlier answer as a mistake. The novel never gives the reader an original contract capable of settling the dispute. Administrative procedures nonetheless begin to move around the role. K ties his claims to lodging, employment and admission to the castle to the name “land surveyor.”[^castle-role]

The role first gives him a timeline. A present refusal can mean that the paperwork remains incomplete. Today’s humiliation can be exchanged for future recognition. Anyone with a connection may become the next point of entry. K sees the contradictions in the village procedures and still devotes much of his energy to proving that he has always had the right to enter. He resists by demanding that the castle honor the role in full.

Barnabas lives a quieter version of the same arrangement. Trained in shoemaking, he can help his father complete orders. Olga places the family’s hope of restored standing in his role as a castle messenger. The office-issued outfit promised to him has yet to arrive, and neither the status nor the route of the assignment is clear. Barnabas has grave doubts. Amalia disapproves of the work and may even despise it. Olga continues to read every contact he has with an official as a chance for the family to approach the castle.[^castle-barnabas]

Olga’s interpretive labor reconnects every delay to the future. The promised outfit remains undelivered, and the source and force of each assignment remain unstable. She still treats the moments Barnabas spends in the offices as evidence of proximity. A definite failure would force a change of direction. Once every gap receives an interpretation, protracted suspension lets the future role keep command of the present. The more labor Olga invests in confirmation, the harder it becomes to treat the next errand as something Barnabas can simply set aside.

A career plan arranges time for a practice. A future role gives meaning to an entire life. The difference emerges when the job title changes. Does the original question remain worth pursuing? Can acquired abilities move into another position? Does self-worth collapse with the title? Olga places each of Barnabas’s errands inside a future in which the family regains its status, making the shoemaker’s work in front of them look like daily maintenance that must simply be endured.

In Slavoj Žižek’s reading of Jacques Lacan, a master-signifier temporarily quilts floating signifiers and their meanings, while fantasy supplies desire with coordinates and a scene.[^zizek-signifier] In these novels, “PhD,” “surveyor” and “messenger” join results, family expectations and a life that has yet to happen into one route. The future role seems to issue orders backward in time, telling the present what to sacrifice, whom to compete with and which kinds of work deserve higher standing.

An occupational name can do other work. If the identity of doctor, teacher, engineer or editor brings someone into sustained practice, failure returns from the patient, student, machine or text. The role becomes a starting point for responsibility. K’s appointment and Olga’s investment in the messenger post lack that return. As a future position grows in importance, the object at hand becomes material for reaching it.

This future tense has also entered educational governance. A Ministry of Education directive on graduate employment requires institutions to link employment outcomes to admissions and training, issue red- and yellow-card warnings for programs with poor employment quality, and incorporate career education into degree programs.[^edu-employment] The policy treats the fit between supply and demand as an object of governance. It also places future jobs inside decisions about programs, resources and curricula.

When the role precedes the object and the subject can receive recognition only through the role, a job title defines success and explains failure. Fang Hongjian knows his doctorate is empty. K argues with the procedures every day, and Olga repeatedly acknowledges the ambiguity of the messenger assignment. Yet the credential, appointment and assignment keep organizing conduct within their households. A future role with no accomplished task cannot prove itself through results. Examiners, rankings, files and appointments must confirm it. The more completely a future position organizes someone’s life, the more closely that person must read these documents and rankings. Whoever confirms the role also gains the authority to end the argument.

## On the Third Reading, Zhou Jin Suddenly Finds a “Masterpiece”

Serving as an examiner, Zhou Jin reads Fan Jin’s essay once and cannot make sense of it. On the second reading, he begins to see its sense. By the third, he judges it an essay of supreme merit and passes Fan Jin. Behind the script he sees an aging candidate who has gone unrecognized for decades, and in Fan Jin he recognizes his own history. Personal sympathy enters the reading. Zhou Jin’s office as examiner turns that reading into the fact of admission.[^scholars-fan]

Neither discernment nor favoritism can dispose of this scene by itself. The novel supplies no independent standard with which a reader outside the examination hall could issue a final judgment on the essay. Zhou Jin’s impulse to compensate Fan Jin for old injuries does not cancel the care of his repeated reading. The third reading ends the dispute because the examiner’s authority writes it into the roll. Everyone who comes later need only read the result.

*The Castle* breaks the examiner with a face into a series of offices. The village head explains the origin of K’s appointment. The village had already replied that it needed no surveyor, yet the papers went to the wrong department. The relevant file may have remained in the village or vanished in transit. The department handling the mistaken delivery eventually received nothing but an empty file cover. The inquiry into this mistake produced still more documents. No official has the whole sequence in view, and no one will declare the entire procedure void. Each office confirms only what it received and what it sent onward.[^castle-files]

![In a village office filled with files, an official shows an empty file cover to a surveyor seen from behind while hands at the frame’s edges continue to pass in papers.](./when-credentials-live-for-us/castle-empty-file-cover.webp)

*Original conceptual illustration: the mistake in the appointment is dispersed among files and offices, leaving each custodian with only one part. Not a still from an adaptation of “The Castle.”*

This dispersal increases the interpretive labor required of those caught in the procedure, and it enlarges authority. The reversal over the telephone may come from a higher office. A missing document may already belong to another review. The ignorance of the clerk at hand can mean that knowledge resides farther inside. The harder the procedure becomes to recount, the harder it is for one contradiction to end it. K and the villagers must keep guessing which sheet of paper lies closer to the castle’s intention.

The procedure acquires a peculiar reliability. Individual answers conflict, and participants doubt the force of telephone calls and letters. The villagers still use them to arrange lodging, employment and obedience. They assume that some position knows more, even as that position recedes with every encounter. Zhou Jin’s office gives the essay its value. The castle files make judgment look as though it has already occurred somewhere and simply has yet to reach the village.

Žižek’s term “objective belief” describes this separation. Belief can reside in rituals, objects and social action; private reservation does not bring it to an end.[^zizek-belief] K can ridicule the procedure, and the village head can admit that the archive is a mess. As long as they continue to allocate housing, work and relationships through the appointment, the belief already resides in those actions. The castle need not find a person convinced of its infallibility. These acts maintain the appointment’s force.

Externalization changes what the judge has to answer for. Zhou Jin must make a choice on the script by hand, leaving at least one examiner who can be questioned. A castle official can cite the previous document, while the village head describes his own decision as the passive continuation of an administrative history. The judgment retains its effects as the person who made it becomes harder to find. Credential holders meet a succession of intermediaries, each claiming to lack final authority. The argument has no opponent.

Zhou Jin can at least reread the script. Every correction in the castle produces another procedure, until error becomes a reason for the procedure to continue.

After recounting the archival history, the village head returns to village life. He says the boundaries have long been surveyed and registered, land seldom changes hands, and the village itself resolves the few boundary disputes. It has no need for a surveyor. The procedure confirms K’s position, while the land gives him no work.

## Klamm Praises a Survey That Never Happened

Barnabas later brings a second letter attributed to Klamm. It praises the surveying K has completed, commends his assistants and urges him to continue the work. K replies that he has never surveyed any land and apparently never will. Pointing to his supposed assistants, he tells Barnabas to judge their worth for himself. The village has no need for a surveyor and K has no results. A favorable assessment from above has already arrived.[^castle-praise]

The novel never tells us what evidence supports the letter. Placed beside the appointment and the assistants, it lets the procedure appear to certify itself. The role of “surveyor” comes first, assistants are attached to it, and a supervisory letter can already read progress from the arrangement. The land stays outside the circuit. The apparatus of evaluation has finally secured an object: the record it has produced itself.

Reality subjects work to heavy-footed tests. A survey must agree with the land, shoes must reach the customer, and dirt should disappear after a floor is cleaned. The objects of intellectual work resist in more complicated ways. Textual evidence defeats an elegant interpretation. A student’s failure to understand forces a teacher to change course. People affected by a policy can identify the consequences omitted by a model.

Researchers choose concepts and teachers design courses; their actions also affect how an object becomes visible. A test occurs when a method meets resistance that credentials and procedures cannot absorb in advance. If evidence and affected people may answer only through predetermined options, the apparatus hears its own language.

Evaluation helps organizers see work across large collaborations. Credits, reviews, projects and performance records give public form to activities that cannot all be watched firsthand. When pay, promotion or institutional survival depends on these records, while failure exposed by the object cannot revise them, attention keeps moving toward what can be entered into the record. A form asks for progress, so participants produce something that fits its progress field. A review rewards one kind of career, so institutions reproduce that career. Anomalies continue to occur without gaining the authority to alter the assessment.

Some failures in the humanities do not announce themselves like a machine stoppage or a scrapped product. Peer judgment and linguistic form therefore bear more of the mediating burden. Slow feedback still has consequences. A misreading can hide a contradiction in a text. An empty course can leave students with nothing except credits. Research that supplies power with elegant phrasing changes what the public can say. An intellectual who accepts peer recognition as final inspection can keep producing words while losing contact with these effects.

Ma Er’s work editing model examination essays shows how this closure enters intellectual production. He reads scripts, annotates them and publishes selections; the publisher puts books in the hands of real readers. Their principal use is to teach the next cohort how to make the same evaluative rule recognize them. Knowledge continues to be produced, but production circles credentialing and returns to reinforce it. Reading time, exercises and rewards rearrange themselves around the examiner’s taste.

Such a circuit can generate many visible results. Better model collections let candidates reproduce the rule more reliably. Those admitted move into examination and teaching, where their own success confirms the value of the collections. The institution does not have to fabricate every result. It only has to let successful candidates define success in the next round. The work can be real, the books popular and the participants diligent while evaluation drifts away from its object.

K later accepts a post as school caretaker. One morning the pupils and teacher have already crowded into the room. K sweeps and changes the water while Frieda scrubs the floor. The village head meant the post to contain and discipline a troublesome outsider, but maintaining the schoolhouse still confronts them with actual dust, dirty water and users. None of these recognizes the surveyor’s identity or reads Klamm’s letter. K takes on work with an object and continues to treat it as a demotion endured while he waits for formal recognition.[^castle-school]

Klamm’s praise obtains confirmation by passing among documents. Cleaning must answer to the dust and dirty water in the schoolhouse. A procedure that lets internal records alone determine success can recognize a role after the object of work has disappeared.

When feedback from affected people cannot reach an address that forces everyone to judge again, responsibility disperses along two kinds of chain. Organizations can split action among forms, superiors and local duties. Participants in markets and neighborhoods respond to immediate risks and to their expectations of others. The injured party experiences the whole result while each actor sees one link. Amalia’s family in *The Castle* exposes the second chain. *The Scholars* supplies a shorter administrative one.

## No Verdict, Yet Amalia’s Family Has Already Been Punished

In the family history Olga tells K, the castle official Sortini once summoned Amalia in a vulgar and threatening letter. She tore it up, refused to go to the inn and performed no later ritual of submission. Daily life begins to impose sanctions without an investigation or a verdict.[^castle-amalia]

Customers collect the boots they had left for repair, and entries in the order book are crossed out one by one. The assistant Brunswick leaves and establishes his own business. Friends and acquaintances withdraw. The Fire Brigade Association dismisses the father and reclaims his certificate. Relatives later send covert aid, but those contacts cannot sustain the income, reputation and social relations the family has lost. The father goes from office to office seeking pardon. Officials tell him that the public file contains no accusation. Since he cannot name a formal penalty, nothing exists for them to pardon or rescind.

The castle itself performs little of the action in this history. Sortini sends the summons; villagers carry most of its subsequent force. Olga attributes their retreat to fear but cannot identify a command that unifies their acts. The consequences borne by the family are complete, as though everyone had executed a verdict that never existed.

Mutual expectations can connect these acts. If villagers expect others to shun the family, maintaining contact alone looks dangerous. Each withdrawal becomes evidence to the next person that the matter has been settled. Everyone watches everyone else and reads the actions of others as proof of the castle’s will. Olga gives no definite sequence of transmission. Punishment may still spread through these expectations while the center remains absent.

A customer can leave boots with the father or take them away. An assistant can stay or leave his employer to bear the risk. The Fire Brigade’s decision-makers never try the case, yet dismissal and the withdrawal of the certificate add their force to the punishment. The actors possess different degrees of freedom and knowledge. Responsibility cannot be divided equally, but those differences do not remove them from the causal chain.

Chapter 4 of *The Scholars* presents a chain of duties. Zhang Jingzhai advises Magistrate Tang to punish severely a teacher who, backed by members of his religious community, comes to plead and brings beef. The magistrate orders the penalty, and the runners execute it by placing the man in the cangue. He eventually dies. Fan Jin, who is present, mainly corrects Zhang’s mistake about ranks in the examination system. Advice, command, execution and spectatorship carry different weights. They still form a continuous sequence that ends in a death. Assigning the entire death to Fan Jin would distort the episode. His indifference to actual suffering still belongs in the account of responsibility.[^scholars-punishment]

Dennis Thompson calls situations in which many people participate in different ways in one organizational decision or outcome, obscuring individual contributions, “the problem of many hands.” Attribution requires us to track each person’s causal contribution, knowledge and degree of coercion.[^thompson] Those lines also distinguish the local acts in the novels. Sortini issues the summons; customers cut off income; the Fire Brigade’s decision-makers create a public stigma; neighbors face different costs when they refuse. Combining them into “the village” hides those differences again.

Punishment without a case file also deprives its targets of the means to assemble what happened into a case. The father cannot identify a decision, a date or a signature. Officials can therefore break his losses back down into private transactions and neighborhood relations. Power leaves the effects in lived experience while removing the decision from the record. The victims know that punishment exists, but struggle to turn their dispersed encounters into a common fact that can be challenged in public.

Roles supply a language for narrowing vision. Officials check only the public file, and the Fire Brigade records the dismissal as an internal decision. Customers retrieve their boots, and an assistant leaves his employer. The first group of acts disappears behind the boundary of official duty; the second disappears behind private choice. Both chains strand the family’s answer between local actions. Amalia’s family has no place where all the participants must face the consequences together.

Once no one acknowledges a public punishment as a public decision, it takes on the appearance of an environment. Villagers see only the risk of continued association; the father sees refusal at each new window. An environment seems to have no author and is therefore hard to contest collectively as a rule. For those living inside it, protecting one’s own point of entry often becomes the most practicable action.

## K Sees a Problem of Power, Then Returns to His Own Way In

When Olga first tells K about the summons, he describes Sortini’s act as an abuse of power that could threaten everyone. He has not yet heard how the villagers shunned the family or how the father later sought pardon across the offices. The letter first makes a public problem visible: an official’s private demand borrows enough weight from the castle for the whole village to fear it.[^castle-k-turn]

Olga continues her account of Amalia and Sortini, and compares Amalia with Frieda. K later concedes that the affair has changed shape in his eyes. Housing, position, employment, marriage, village membership and access to Klamm regain the center. He keeps listening and asking questions about the family history, but now weighs each detail along another axis. Amalia is Barnabas’s sister, and Barnabas may bring K closer to Klamm.

K never stops acting. He argues with the village head, refuses to give a deposition to Momus, waits for Klamm in the snow, and uses lodging, marriage and the messenger connection in his search for an opening. He sees the absurdity of the procedure more clearly than many villagers do. All this energy still runs through possessives: his appointment and rights, his own way in. Seeing through the institution does not take him outside those coordinates. It makes him more adept at moving within them.

When no author can be found for a decision and no address for an appeal, application materials become the only surface an individual can alter directly. One applicant cannot rewrite admissions quotas, labor regimes or urban boundaries. They can sit the examination again, change fields, add a certificate or enter another institution. When the allocation of resources reaches people only as a fixed threshold, and collective challenge has no accessible entrance or carries too high a price, agency concentrates on admission and mobility.

When exit displaces voice and route knowledge becomes the chief inheritance, personal adaptation accumulates into a more durable environment. Successful candidates tell those behind them which certificate carries more value and how to avoid risk. Shared conditions receive less discussion. The next cohort sees a route already validated by others, giving them more reason to treat self-modification as the available action.

If a model’s aims, assumptions and allocation of error costs remain closed to challenge, the individual receives only its final threshold. Supply-and-demand forecasts govern program design; employers set barriers through risk control; organizational penalties arrive as the outcome of a process. The people who choose the objective and bear the costs of error disappear behind models and forms. The individual receives a checklist of documents.

The allocation of educational resources, the recognition of labor and the losses imposed by organizational decisions all concern common rules. By the time they reach an individual, they may appear only as questions about whether a field is popular, whether an application is complete and which route is safer. Political detachment occurs in this translation. A person can monitor rankings, hiring and promotion with great sensitivity while keeping a distance from the power that sets their standards. Public questions become information and planning, and politics contracts to the dimensions of a résumé.

Repeated examinations, meticulous information gathering and fierce self-discipline can all carry political detachment. They also produce a keen sense for an organization’s micropolitics: who controls the openings, which office can sign, and how a résumé becomes visible.

People rely on career choice, migration and examinations for their livelihoods, and all three can coexist with public action. Dependence on a role fixes the world as a set of given conditions and spends all of the subject’s effort on improving their place within them. K once understood Sortini’s summons as a general problem of power. As Olga’s account develops, he sorts the history again by housing, position, marriage and access to Klamm. His action contracts into route calculation.

A personal route can also produce a sense of moral cleanliness. The customer handles a transaction, the official checks a file and the applicant revises a dossier. No one considers themselves the author of the rule, so the consequences return to “the organization” or “the market.” Once politics retreats into planning, the subject need neither avow the institution nor judge their part in its continued operation. Moving to a better position becomes the most verifiable form of success.

K has long known that telephone calls are unreliable, files disappear and superiors evaluate work that does not exist. This knowledge gives him no practice outside the role, and it gives the villagers no place to address punishment together. Breaking the chain requires reality to regain the right to answer. Participants also need an address at which dispersed consequences return to common judgment.

## A Pair of Shoes Keeps the Messenger at Home

When Barnabas delivers another letter attributed to Klamm, K asks about the private message he had previously entrusted to him. Barnabas says that on the day after receiving K’s message he did not go to the castle. His father had too much shoemaking to finish and needed his help. K is furious. In his ordering of work, a message that might reach Klamm outranks the father’s craft. The conversation compresses two schedules. One leads toward castle messages whose source and force remain unstable. The other stays with the orders waiting in a shoemaker’s hands.[^castle-barnabas]

Shoemaking offers none of the castle’s glory. An order eventually encounters leather, measurements and use. A title cannot complete it. The object retains a voice inside this circuit. The messenger’s work has yet to acquire the promised office-issued outfit or defined rights, yet Olga reads each ambiguous contact as another step toward recognition.

![In a winter shoemaker’s workshop, a father and adult son measure a shoe at the bench while leather and tools lie before them and a sealed letter rests to one side.](./when-credentials-live-for-us/castle-shoemaker-order.webp)

*Original conceptual illustration: a messenger post can remain suspended, but leather, fit and use demand an answer from the craft at hand. Not a still from an adaptation of “The Castle.”*

K and Frieda enter the other circuit while cleaning the school. Pupils and the teacher already fill the room. K sweeps and changes the water while Frieda scrubs the floor. Everyone can see who did the work and what happened to the dirt and dirty water. A division of labor does not dissolve responsibility by itself, and low-status work still has a structure of production. K treats the caretaker’s duties as a temporary demotion. Feedback from an object can correct a task without reorganizing someone’s desire.

Amalia preserves her own judgment. She tears up the summons and refuses to buy safety with formal submission. The family’s isolation marks the limit of an individual refusal. Her act exposes power’s reliance on everyone else’s cooperation. It cannot create a procedure for appeal or compel customers and neighbors to share the risk.

These actions do not acquire the force to rewrite common rules. *The Castle* offers practice and refusal without a mature political community. Treating the isolated dissenter’s cost as a ready answer would let another role perform our judgment for us.

Poor shoemaking ruins an order; a text that cannot withstand a counterexample fails, and a degree cannot supply content its author has not written. Objects can overturn an initial credential. They cannot decide who chooses the order, who designs the course or who bears the cost of failure. However well K cleans the schoolhouse, Amalia still has nowhere to appeal. Her refusal reveals whose action the order depends on. The villagers’ ensuing isolation of the family shows what bystanders learn when no one bears risk in common: protect your own position more carefully.

A credential can preserve the record of a period of training. Practice must still submit it to the text, the student, the customer and the affected person. A degree says that an author received training; an academic title records a judgment once made by peers. One counterexample can defeat the next article. When knowledge circulates only among projects, reviews and citations, the author may remain exceptionally busy while the world loses its means of forcing them to retract a conclusion.

A customer can point to the failure in a shoe that does not fit. Amalia’s father cannot point to a decision, date or signature. Complex divisions of labor still need addresses of responsibility where affected people can appeal, and where finishing one link cannot end scrutiny of the overall consequences. Once conflict returns to such a common address, participants may change a rule instead of changing routes.

The notice hanging in Fan Jin’s house preserves one decision to admit him. It cannot write the essays he has yet to produce or determine how he will treat his family and the people he governs. Butcher Hu, Zhang Jingzhai and the neighbors live the notice into social reality. Later action can still revoke its right to final judgment.

Olga can interpret the castle letter in Barnabas’s hand again and again, turning every delay into another promised future. Any shoemaker’s order eventually meets leather, measurements and use. The castle’s procedures cannot perform that inspection or postpone it forever.
