## The Notice Made Fan Jin a “Master” Before He Did

Fan Jin stands in the market holding a chicken. His family has run out of food, and his mother has sent him to sell the bird for rice. The men carrying the examination results enter the village while he is away. His neighbors search for him and finally drag home a man who still refuses to believe the news. The notice already hangs in the house. Fan Jin sees his name, decades of examinations come due on a single sheet of paper, and his body gives way. By the time Butcher Hu slaps him out of his delirium, everyone around him has begun to observe a different etiquette.[^scholars-fan]

Butcher Hu once treated his son-in-law as a penniless pedant. Now he hardly dares touch this incarnation of the Literary Star. He straightens Fan Jin’s clothes and follows with his head bowed. Zhang Jingzhai claims kinship through an old teacher-pupil connection, calls Fan Jin a brother, and gives him money and a house. Forms of address change alongside kinship and access to resources. Fan Jin has yet to write another essay or perform a single official duty. The notice has already assigned actions to everyone else.

The credential has already taken material form in the money, the house and the butcher’s conduct. Those around Fan Jin respond to the notice, altering the relationships and resources now open to him. Before Fan Jin can decide who he has become, the community has believed the notice for him.

Even before the notice arrives, the examination has given knowledge its shape. Mr Ma Er surveys the routes open to learned men from the Spring and Autumn period through Song Neo-Confucianism, then ends with the present dynasty’s selection by examination essays. If the sages lived today, he says, they too would have to write the prescribed form before entering office. After years of failure in the examinations, Ma Er earns a living by reading scripts and editing model essays for publishers. What to read, how to read it and how to judge it all return to the kind of essay an examiner will recognize.[^scholars-ma]

Long exam training familiarizes students with a durable relation. Someone else poses the question, an authorized judge assesses the answer, an institution preserves the rank, and the prize is a future social position. When this circuit occupies the main hours of study and the principal channel of success and failure, the authority to pose the problem stays outside the examination hall. Students devote more of their effort to adapting to the problem already set.

Rankings turn that adaptation into differences between people. One mistake belongs to a moment, but a series of records can rewrite it as something “this person is bad at.” A high score can expand just as easily into a general judgment about discipline, intelligence and prospects. Schools, families and employers save the cost of learning who someone is afresh. The people being judged learn to describe themselves through the same record. Even doubt and redirection have to be explained against the curve of their scores.

In *Discipline and Punish*, Michel Foucault places the examination at the intersection of hierarchical observation and normalizing judgment. Names, marks, comments and ranks turn a person into a “case” that can be described, compared and retrieved.[^foucault-exam] After the scripts have been collected, that case can still move into marriage, employment and ordinary association, telling strangers how to treat the person in the record.

Standardization widens that record’s circulation. Ronald Dore’s account of the “diploma disease” identifies two joined institutional conditions: standardized examinations give certificates a common currency, and career bureaucracies use general educational qualifications to select future members.[^dore] A certificate now links a present record to a future position. An organization uses it as a provisional answer to what an applicant can already do and as a basis for deciding which occupational route to place them on.

A credential can therefore operate like credit secured against future performance. Zhang Jingzhai gives Fan Jin a house before he takes office, investing in the power and connections a successful candidate may later acquire. Modern organizations need not wait for every applicant to perform the job before making an initial selection. This lowers the cost of selection at scale and gives one educational record a social lifespan far longer than the examination itself.

Once an institution preserves and repeatedly calls upon a judgment, the record crosses the boundary of the examination and takes possession of a name. The notice first answers “Who is Fan Jin?” It then promises the life toward which that name will lead. A past result acquires the tense of the future.

## A Position Sent Back from the Future

Fang Hongjian spends several years studying in Europe without completing a degree he can show for them. As his return approaches, he needs a visible result to present to his father and his late fiancée’s family. He buys a doctorate from the fictitious Carleton University. The purchase adds nothing to his research; the “foreign-trained PhD” arrives home ahead of him. His elders can exhibit a return on their educational investment, and local society receives a ready-made name for him.[^fortress]

Fang knows the degree is fraudulent and keeps a private rule against listing it formally when applying for work. The photograph, the newspaper report and his relatives have already taken possession of the title. His shame preserves some distance from the role. Those around him have already pictured his respectable career and marriage prospects.

*The Castle* carries the same structure from credential to appointment. K arrives in the village late at night and, when threatened with expulsion, says that the Count has summoned him to serve as land surveyor. A telephone call first denies that the village has any such surveyor, then recognizes the earlier answer as a mistake. The novel never displays an original contract capable of settling the dispute; administrative procedures already move around the role. K ties his claims to lodging, employment and admission to the castle to the name “land surveyor.”[^castle-role]

The role first gives him a timeline. A present refusal can mean that the paperwork remains incomplete. Today’s humiliation can be exchanged for future recognition. Anyone with a connection may become the next point of entry. K sees the contradictions in the village procedures and still devotes much of his energy to proving that he has always had the right to enter. He resists by demanding that the castle honor the role in full.

Barnabas is trained in shoemaking and can help his father complete orders. Olga places the family’s hope of restored standing in his role as a castle messenger. The promised office-issued outfit has yet to arrive, and neither the status nor the route of the assignment is clear. She continues to read every contact he has with an official as evidence that the family is approaching the castle.[^castle-barnabas]

Every delay can be reconnected to the future. The missing outfit may mean that recognition is still on its way; an ambiguous errand may be a trial before formal appointment. The more Olga invests in confirmation, the harder it becomes to set aside the next errand as an ordinary task. The father’s shoemaking is reduced to the present work of subsistence, while the messenger post takes possession of the family’s future.

This future tense has also entered educational governance. A Ministry of Education directive on graduate employment requires institutions to link employment outcomes to admissions and training, issue red- and yellow-card warnings for programs with poor employment quality, and incorporate career education into degree programs.[^edu-employment] The policy treats the fit between supply and demand as an object of governance. It also places future jobs inside decisions about programs, resources and curricula.

Employment policy connects data about graduate positions back to admissions and resource allocation; the roles in these novels insert the future into household life. Fang’s shame, K’s mockery of procedure and Barnabas’s doubts remain private. Relatives and villagers still arrange their conduct around the PhD, the surveyor and the messenger. With no work product to inspect, they keep reading diplomas, appointments and letters.

## On the Third Reading, Zhou Jin Suddenly Finds a “Masterpiece”

Serving as an examiner, Zhou Jin reads Fan Jin’s essay once and cannot make sense of it. On the second reading, he begins to see its sense. By the third, he judges it an essay of supreme merit and passes Fan Jin. Behind the script he sees an aging candidate who has gone unrecognized for decades, and in Fan Jin he recognizes his own history. Personal sympathy enters the reading. Zhou Jin’s office as examiner turns that reading into the fact of admission.[^scholars-fan]

Neither discernment nor favoritism can dispose of this scene by itself. The novel supplies no independent standard with which a reader outside the examination hall could issue a final judgment on the essay. Zhou Jin’s impulse to compensate Fan Jin for old injuries does not cancel the care of his repeated reading. The third reading ends the dispute because the examiner’s authority writes it into the roll. Everyone who comes later need only read the result.

*The Castle* breaks the examiner with a face into a series of offices. The village head explains the origin of K’s appointment. The village had already replied that it needed no surveyor, yet the papers went to the wrong department. The relevant file may have remained in the village or vanished in transit. The department handling the mistaken delivery eventually received nothing but an empty file cover. The inquiry into this mistake produced still more documents. No official has the whole sequence in view, and no one will declare the entire procedure void. Each office confirms only what it received and what it sent onward.[^castle-files]

![In a village office filled with files, an official shows an empty file cover to a surveyor seen from behind while hands at the frame’s edges continue to pass in papers.](./when-credentials-live-for-us/castle-empty-file-cover.webp)

*Original conceptual illustration: the mistake in the appointment is dispersed among files and offices, leaving each custodian with only one part. Not a still from an adaptation of “The Castle.”*

A missing document may already belong to another review. The ignorance of the clerk at hand may mean that knowledge resides farther inside. K and the villagers keep guessing which sheet of paper lies closer to the castle’s intention. They doubt the telephone calls and letters, then use the appointment to allocate housing and work. Žižek’s term “objective belief” names this separation: belief can reside in rituals, objects and social action, and private doubt does not stop it.[^zizek-belief]

Zhou Jin must make a choice on the script by hand, leaving an examiner who can be questioned. Castle officials cite earlier documents, and the village head describes decisions as continuations of administrative history. The judgment loses its author as it passes among intermediaries. Zhou Jin can reread the script; every correction in the castle produces another procedure.

Only after recounting the archive does the village head return to the land. Its boundaries have long been surveyed, land seldom changes hands, and the village resolves the few disputes itself. The procedure confirms K’s position. The land gives him no work.

## Klamm Praises a Survey That Never Happened

Barnabas later brings a second letter attributed to Klamm. It praises the surveying K has completed, commends his assistants and urges him to continue the work. K replies that he has never surveyed any land and apparently never will. Pointing to his supposed assistants, he tells Barnabas to judge their worth for himself. The village has no need for a surveyor and K has no results. A favorable assessment from above has already arrived.[^castle-praise]

Read against the idle land, the letter suggests an evaluative loop: the appointment produces a surveyor and attaches assistants to him, then a supervisory letter reads progress from those records. The apparatus of evaluation takes its own record as an object and leaves the land outside the circuit.

A survey must agree with the land, shoes must reach the customer, and the dirty water in a classroom must be changed. Intellectual work encounters slower forms of resistance. Textual evidence can defeat an elegant interpretation, students can force a teacher to change course, and people affected by a policy can identify consequences omitted by a model. Their answers enter inspection only when they can alter the record and the method.

Credits, reviews and project records help organizers see work across large collaborations. When pay and promotion depend on the record, while failures exposed by the object cannot revise it, participants are more likely to direct their effort toward what can be entered on a form. Ma Er reads, annotates and publishes model essays that help the next cohort reproduce the examiners’ rules. Successful candidates later enter teaching and examination, where their own success confirms the value of those collections.

K later accepts a post as school caretaker. One morning the pupils and teacher have already crowded into the room. K sweeps and changes the water while Frieda scrubs the floor. Dust and dirty water do not read Klamm’s letter; the pupils and teacher can see who cleaned the room. K treats this work, with an object and affected users, as a demotion endured while he waits to be recognized as surveyor.[^castle-school] The schoolhouse inspects the cleaning. K continues to arrange his life around the surveyor’s future.

When the evaluative circuit closes, the land has nothing to survey and failures exposed by texts or students leave the existing record unchanged. The school cleaning retains a direct circuit of inspection: the pupils and teacher can see whether the dust has been swept and the dirty water replaced. Amalia’s family next encounters a different break. Many people carry out separate parts of the punishment, and the family can find no overall decision against which to appeal.

## No Verdict, Yet Amalia’s Family Has Already Been Punished

In the family history Olga tells K, the castle official Sortini once summoned Amalia in a vulgar and threatening letter. She tore it up, refused to go to the inn and performed no later ritual of submission. Daily life begins to impose sanctions without an investigation or a verdict.[^castle-amalia]

Customers collect the boots they had left for repair, and entries in the order book are crossed out one by one. The assistant Brunswick leaves and establishes his own business. Friends and acquaintances withdraw. The Fire Brigade Association dismisses the father and reclaims his certificate. Relatives later send covert aid, but those contacts cannot sustain the income, reputation and social relations the family has lost. The father goes from office to office seeking pardon. Officials tell him that the public file contains no accusation. Since he cannot name a formal penalty, nothing exists for them to pardon or rescind.

After Sortini sends the summons, the villagers perform most of the punishment. Olga attributes their retreat to fear but cannot identify a command that unifies their acts. The center remains absent while the family experiences the full force of an executed verdict.

Those who come later can read earlier withdrawals as evidence that the matter has been settled, then leave their own withdrawal for the next observer. These mutual expectations spread punishment without a sequence of transmission or a formal command.

Collecting the boots, leaving a master, and withdrawing the Fire Brigade certificate are decisions made by identifiable people. At the level of the act, they know what they are doing and have room to refuse. Olga does not tell us with equal clarity how much each party knows of the castle’s intention or what refusal would cost. Dennis Thompson calls such cases “the problem of many hands.” Attribution requires us to trace causal contribution, knowledge and pressure person by person.[^thompson]

Punishment without a case file also deprives its targets of the means to assemble what happened into a case. The father cannot identify a decision, a date or a signature, so officials break his losses back down into private transactions and neighborhood relations. Officials check the public file; the Fire Brigade records an internal decision; customers collect their boots; an assistant leaves his employer. Each act can be defended in isolation. Amalia’s family has no place where all the participants must face the consequences together.

Once no one acknowledges a public punishment as a public decision, it takes on the appearance of an environment. Villagers see only the risk of continued association; the father sees refusal at each new window. The environment seems to have no author, and those living inside it are more likely to turn toward protecting their own point of entry.

## K Sees a Problem of Power, Then Returns to His Own Way In

The land has no work to survey, yet the letter records progress. Amalia’s family bears the full punishment, while the father can find no overall decision against which to appeal. K first experiences the break between evaluation and its object, then hears from Olga about the break between punishment and decision. When she first tells him about the summons, he describes Sortini’s act as an abuse of power that could threaten everyone. He has not yet heard how the villagers shunned the family or how the father later sought pardon across the offices. The letter first makes a public problem visible: an official’s private demand borrows enough weight from the castle for the whole village to fear it.[^castle-k-turn]

Olga continues her account of Amalia and Sortini, and compares Amalia with Frieda. K later concedes that the affair has changed shape in his eyes. Housing, position, employment, marriage, village membership and access to Klamm regain the center. He keeps listening and asking questions about the family history; the new axis determines how he uses each detail. Amalia is Barnabas’s sister, and Barnabas may bring K closer to Klamm.

K never stops acting. He argues with the village head, refuses to give a deposition to Momus, waits for Klamm in the snow, and uses lodging, marriage and the messenger connection in his search for an opening. He sees the absurdity of the procedure more clearly than many villagers do. All this energy still runs through possessives: his appointment and rights, his own way in. That clarity makes him more adept at moving within the castle’s coordinates.

When no author can be found for a decision and no address for an appeal, application materials become the surface an individual can alter directly. One applicant cannot rewrite admissions quotas or labor regimes and is more likely to sit the examination again, change fields or add a certificate. Institutions keep receiving materials arranged by the old criteria, and some successful candidates pass those criteria to the next cohort. Personal adaptation then reinforces the environment.

The allocation of educational resources, the recognition of labor and the losses imposed by organizational decisions all concern common rules. By the time they reach an individual, they are often compressed into questions about whether a field is popular, whether an application is complete and which route is safer. This translation is what the essay calls political detachment: a person monitors rankings, hiring and promotion closely while remaining distant from the power that sets their standards. Their agency concentrates on entering the next position.

People rely on examinations, migration and career choices for their livelihoods, and these can coexist with public action. The structure becomes closed when all personal effort goes into improving one’s position while the people who set the conditions and those who bear the consequences cannot appear in the same dispute. K already knows that telephone calls are unreliable, files disappear and superiors assess work that does not exist. That knowledge gives him no practice beyond the role and gives the villagers no place to address the punishment of Amalia’s family together.

## A Pair of Shoes Keeps the Messenger at Home

When Barnabas delivers another letter attributed to Klamm, K asks about the private message he had previously entrusted to him. Barnabas says that on the following day he did not go to the castle because his father had too much shoemaking to finish and needed his help. K is furious. In his ordering of work, a message that might reach Klamm outranks the orders in the father’s hands.[^castle-barnabas]

Shoemaking offers none of the castle’s glory. An order eventually encounters leather, measurements and use, and a title cannot complete it. The messenger’s work has yet to acquire the promised office-issued outfit or defined rights, while Olga reads each ambiguous contact as another step toward recognition. At the workbench, an ill-fitting shoe returns failure to its maker.

![In a winter shoemaker’s workshop, a father and adult son measure a shoe at the bench while leather and tools lie before them and a sealed letter rests to one side.](./when-credentials-live-for-us/castle-shoemaker-order.webp)

*Original conceptual illustration: a messenger post can remain suspended; leather, fit and use demand an answer from the craft at hand. Not a still from an adaptation of “The Castle.”*

Amalia tears up the summons and preserves her judgment; the whole family then bears the isolation. The work in her father’s hands disappears order by order: customers collect their boots and the order-book entries are crossed out, the Fire Brigade withdraws his certificate, and neighbors will not bear the risk of continued contact alone. Her refusal exposes the castle’s dependence on this cooperation. As long as each neighbor calculates the risk alone, the family continues to face a punishment with no overall decision.

A credential can preserve a judgment made at the end of training. Once practice begins, texts, students, customers and affected people need the power to revise that judgment. A degree records that an author received training; one counterexample can still defeat the next article. Complex divisions of labor need addresses of responsibility where affected people can appeal and where finishing one link does not end scrutiny of the overall consequence. Politics beyond the personal route begins at such an address.

The notice in Fan Jin’s house preserves one decision to admit him; Butcher Hu, Zhang Jingzhai and the neighbors live it into social reality. Olga can interpret the castle letter in Barnabas’s hand again and again, turning every delay into another promised future. That day the castle gives no answer about Barnabas’s identity. The leather on his father’s bench waits for him to take its measure.
