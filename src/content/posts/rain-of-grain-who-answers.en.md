*The Power of Writing and Self-Formation in the Age of AI*

“In ancient times, Cangjie created writing; grain fell from heaven, and ghosts wept at night.”

The “Basic Warp” chapter of the *Huainanzi* presents the invention of writing as an event that shook heaven and earth. Food falls from the sky; ghosts cry in the darkness. The scene is magnificent and unsettling. A little further on, immediately after the story of Boyi digging wells, comes another sentence: “Abilities grew more numerous, while virtue grew thinner.” In this account, human powers have increased, but an existing order of life has been disturbed. The creation of writing brings with it an anxiety about how people live.[^huainan]

Today, discussions of AI language often begin with its usefulness. It can clarify a muddled thought, restore conditions omitted from an argument, or organize scattered requirements into work that can proceed. These improvements have real value. From such experiences, it is easy to ask a further question: if AI expresses things so thoroughly, should we learn to speak that way too? Should research, writing, practical work, even our understanding of ourselves, all conform to this clearer mode of expression?

While discussing this question with AI, I once adopted a classification of activities. Later, I asked whether we still needed it as an independent layer, given that we could already analyze objects, conditions, and norms through concrete practices. After discussion, the classification became a reference to use when needed, and the change was written into the record. Subsequent discussions continued to follow that arrangement. A few sentences had gone beyond the conversation: they changed how the next questions would be discussed.

This small episode gives the question of linguistic standards another dimension. Alongside comparing how well two sentences are written, we need to distinguish when a sentence becomes a judgment, when it enters action, and how it becomes a basis for speaking again. When a system can keep responding, organize records, and carry out requests under appropriate conditions, speech acquires a readily available place from which it can continue.

Ancient imaginings of writing make this familiar situation appear strange. Writing was understood as the traces of heaven and earth, capable of summoning spirits, conferring mandates, and participating in government. Those who possessed it thereby gained a certain power and assumed a certain identity. Today’s technologies have their own grounds of efficacy, but the question still deserves close attention: once words have been sent out, what makes them count? And in the answering, who becomes what kind of person?

## How Mountains and Rivers Come to Be on Paper

The preface to the *Diagrams of the Ancient True Forms of the Five Sacred Peaks* begins with mountains and waters: winding and turning, rising and falling, stretching out and folding back. It then compares flowing waters to an energetic brushstroke and describes ridges, clouds, and forests as resembling written forms. Looking down, the divine lord Tianzhen Daojun discerns their patterns and tendencies and names the mountains according to their shapes. Brushstrokes, mountain contours, and names are connected in this account.[^wuyue]

We usually think first of a mountain, then imagine someone drawing and naming it. The preface unsettles that order. The twists of the landscape themselves display forms close to writing, while sacred vision recognizes patterns, tendencies, and names within them. Before a person encounters the diagram, a relationship between writing and the world is already present. Nor can the “true” in a true-form diagram be measured solely by how faithfully it reduces terrain to a smaller scale.

This relationship also reaches the person carrying the diagram. The preface describes the effects of the five peaks’ forms individually, promising to settle the spirit and prolong life, ward off calamities, and assist passage through mountains and rivers. When someone bearing the forms of the five peaks enters the mountains, their gods are even said to come beyond their boundaries to offer welcome. What is held on paper connects with what is encountered outside the body. The diagram travels with the person, who arrives at the mountain to meet a situation already changed by it.

To summarize this as an ancient belief in the power of symbols would pass over what the texts work hardest to explain: where does this power come from, and why can certain forms acquire it? The *Record of the Divine Talismans of the Three Caverns* develops another account.

It speaks of celestial writings, cloud seals, and true writings, describing the emergence of some sacred writing as “the qi of spontaneous, flying mystery, gathering in the void to form writing.” Of talismans it says: “The vital essence of the Dao is spread upon tablets and ink, meeting the vital essence of things.”[^shenfu] Within this cosmology, the formation and operation of writing are connected to the gathering and circulation of qi. Vital essence is distributed through a material inscription and encounters that of the myriad things. Form and medium thus enter the explanation of efficacy.

The text also distinguishes talismans, writings, and diagrams. Talismans take up the configurations of clouds, things, and stars; writings distinguish sounds and phrases and expound their meaning; diagrams depict the forms of spiritual transformation. Each draws on something different, yet the forms can enter into one another: writings contain diagrams, and visual form works together with sound. Replacing them all with a plain-language summary of their “meaning” might make the sentences easier to understand, while failing to preserve the forms and configurations originally endowed with efficacy. A summary explains what the talismans and diagrams convey. What they are believed capable of doing in this world still has to be understood through their own forms.

Their role in bringing things into being can be stated even more directly. Quoting the *Benxiang jing*, the *Record of the Divine Talismans of the Three Caverns* describes true writings refined by fire, their qi transforming into a forest of seven treasures, its branches and leaves becoming purple writing, and its living beings feeding on dew to attain longevity. Writing here enters a process of transformation and generation: visible true writings are connected to the emergence of life and a world. This explanation depends on the work’s religious cosmology. We must first make room for its understanding of reality to see why writing carries such weight within it.

In classical Chinese texts, *wen* (文) already extends beyond verbal expression. In “Tracing the Dao,” the opening chapter of *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, Liu Xie writes of clouds, plants, forest sounds, springs, and rocks. “When form is established, pattern takes shape; when sound is uttered, wen is born”: forms, colors, textures, and sounds all fall within his view. He then turns to human culture, the classics, and moral education, connecting the wen of heaven and earth with human composition.[^yuandao] Mountains and rivers have discernible forms; verbal expression has its modes of organization. Form allows things to appear and people to apprehend something through them.

“Tracing the Dao” and the *Record of the Divine Talismans of the Three Caverns* each make their own argument. Read together, they reveal a question broader than how words express thoughts. How does wen appear, how is it recognized, and how does it enter human life? Natural patterns make forms discernible; the sound and cadence of a composition organize the reader’s experience; sacred true writings participate in generation within a particular cosmology. A shared word brings these different effects together. Understanding them still requires examining each in its own terms.

Such distinctions also change what we expect from standardized expression. An equation, a diagram, and a rhythmic passage of prose may not be replaceable by the same clear paraphrase. Where paraphrase helps understanding, it is worth making. Where it changes the object we need, we must return to the original form. Researchers sometimes need to see a diagram, readers of poetry sometimes need to sound it out, and people doing practical work sometimes need to perform an operation themselves. To take language in a broader sense is also to attend to what these forms each retain, and to what changes in translating between them.

The true-form diagrams leave a more specific question. If the forms on a diagram can bring mountain gods out to welcome its bearer, who may carry it?

## What a Sheet Confers

Reading on in the *Diagrams of the Ancient True Forms of the Five Sacred Peaks*, we encounter detailed arrangements for inscription, ritual purification, sacrificial announcements, and transmission. The text is quite specific about how a diagram is to reach someone’s hands.

Its transmission prayers use “so-and-so” to reserve places to be filled in: the year and month in which the transmitter previously received the diagram, where and from which former master, and the new recipient’s place of origin, name, and age. The transmitter must state the grounds of the lineage, select a suitable person, and confer the diagram through written oaths and declarations of covenant. The recipient, too, is given a statement to make: a willingness to correct faults, cultivate their practice, safeguard the diagram, and request that their name be entered in sacred registers.[^wuyue] These places awaiting particulars join a portable object to a person who must be identified.

Viewed from here, the earlier welcome by the gods takes on a different significance. The recipient can enter new relationships and becomes someone bound by obligations within them. The power acquired comes with a lineage, commitments, and cultivation. How it is to be used and later transmitted to others is already raised at the moment of reception. The diagram’s efficacy unfolds within this religious life, in which its bearer participates.

The two parties to transmission each have something to do. One must explain where their authority comes from and why they may transmit; the other must make a statement and accept requirements for future conduct. Putting one person’s words in the other’s position would not automatically accomplish the same thing. The content of the writing matters, but the position from which it is spoken and the accompanying actions also help determine what it becomes here.

Xunzi’s “Rectifying Names” approaches the relationship between writing and people from elsewhere. It worries that confusion between names and actual things will prevent people from understanding one another’s intentions, leaving affairs obstructed and neglected. It therefore calls for “distinguishing and establishing names to point to actual things.”[^zhengming] How to discern things and how to work together meet in the problem of naming.

What constrains a name? Xunzi begins with the discernment of the senses, the body, and the heart-mind. Shapes, colors, textures, qualities of sound, sweetness and bitterness, cold and heat must be perceived before sameness and difference can be discussed. Names acquire appropriateness through convention and use. “Names have no fixed appropriateness; they are assigned by agreement, and what becomes established by shared usage is called appropriate.” This is followed by a further claim: “Names do have inherent goodness; those that are direct, easy, and not at odds with things are called good names.” However a name was first chosen, common use and ease of understanding provide grounds for assessing it.

Convention thus carries weight in two directions. Names can be formed and changed; we should not mistake an existing name for an eternal label built into the thing itself. Yet a name already in common use is involved in how people recognize, anticipate, and act. Renaming cannot be merely a matter of one person’s pleasure. Distinguishing a difference calls for perception and reasons; enabling others to act on that distinction also requires mutually intelligible usage.

Xunzi’s order has an explicit hierarchical requirement: names are established “above, to make clear the noble and the lowly; below, to distinguish sameness and difference.” This cannot be quietly recast as modern negotiation among equals. Names help distinguish things and assign people their places. These functions can support one another while also making the worth of retaining a name a matter for dispute. A system of terms that helped administer affairs in its time does not thereby place its hierarchy beyond question.

The transmission of true-form diagrams and Xunzi’s rectification of names show different grounds for writing’s efficacy. One belongs to sacred transmission and cultivation; the other connects perception, convention, and political order. These widely separated accounts both bring the user’s position into the question of writing.

In the life envisaged by the transmission prayers, someone receives a sheet of images and writing, then must cultivate themselves, act in accordance with it, and change toward the life it holds out. The paper already contains prescriptions; subsequent practice must still realize them. The power acquired is connected to how that person enters these relationships and assumes their requirements.

Sending a sentence to AI today also involves an intermediate process that is easy to omit. Why can it become an actual modification, and why might it remain effective after the conversation ends? To see this, we need to return to an instance of use that actually occurred.

## Words Return from the Window

That discussion began with an attempt to distinguish how I used language, and how I handed work over to AI, across writing, problem analysis, research, engineering, and everyday affairs. AI proposed seven kinds of activity, and I agreed to develop the discussion along those lines. Classification gave names to things that had been mixed together, providing an entry into the discussion.

As the analysis developed, concrete practices could already accommodate objects, technical conditions, social relationships, and norms. The place of activity classification became a question. I asked:

> Very good, but there is something we need to consider: once we have practice as our basis, do we still need distinctions between activities?

What I wanted to question was whether those distinctions still needed to be carried by an independent classification. AI then proposed a revision: use concrete practices as the main point of entry, retain “activity” as an ordinary word for describing what is done, and use classifications when they actually help. At this point it was still a proposal. I continued:

> Begin making the corresponding changes.

The relevant records were then revised to make practice the point of entry and remove the independent, mandatory layer of activity classification. The classification itself remained an optional reference. The subsequent discussion of Chinese culture and organization of the theory continued to use the revised arrangement.[^conversation]

The change in this process was small enough to be seen clearly. The first sentence questioned a necessity; the response articulated a possible revision; the second sentence requested implementation; the revised text then entered subsequent discussion. These steps connected, while each did something different. In particular, the “corresponding” in “Begin making the corresponding changes” drew its content from the preceding discussion. Taken on its own, the sentence would specify no determinate change to make.

The record accomplished a transformation within this process. A newly proposed arrangement was initially an object of discussion. Once adopted and saved, it became a condition for further discussion. Where to begin and which classifications to use as needed no longer had to be debated at every opening. Something discussed had entered the background on which discussion continued.

Once formed, that background could affect which questions became easy to see. Beginning with practice allows the analysis to ask first what object is being handled and what relationships and constraints are involved, then whether a classification is needed. Placing classification first can incline a person to search for the appropriate category. The two approaches need not always yield different conclusions, but they already order attention differently. What I can directly confirm is a change in the record and in the way subsequent discussions proceeded. Whether my thinking had already changed accordingly, or which step produced an inward change, cannot be determined from this record alone.

Books, notes, teachers, and shared work have long enabled the results of discussion to enter life. Writing down an agreement, revising a plan, and later acting on it did not begin with AI. The new question here concerns how easy continuation becomes, and how many previously separate actions can be connected within one interaction.

If a system has access only to the present exchange, its response may not carry over into the next one. A record gains a continuing role only if the system can read it and actually works from it. If the system can only generate text, a person must make the changes separately. If, with authorization and appropriate tools, it can write changes back to files, discussion can lead more directly to implementation. Different arrangements have different consequences; a general label such as AI cannot stand in for these differences.

Under such an arrangement, a proposal is quickly discussed and revised, the revision enters the record, and the record continues to influence the next answer. The user is asking how to do something while also forming, together with the system, ways of asking and doing. Once this continuity becomes established, more may change than the time required to finish a task.

For example, someone may previously have made little effort to organize their questions because organizing them was itself too costly. If another person or a system can continually help distinguish problems and preserve leads, allowing something not yet clearly expressed to be investigated further, that person may begin work they had not begun before. What deserves examination then is the new practice: which objects enter view, which capacities become repeatedly available, and which dependencies become necessary conditions. This requires attention to the actual process; greater efficiency alone cannot answer it.

Lower costs can explain why such use becomes feasible. To discuss whether a quantitative change has become a qualitative one, we must examine what has happened to the existing practice. Has help called upon occasionally become a regular condition for generating problems, organizing judgments, or continuing action? If only the speed of the same process changes, the conclusion can stop there. If how work unfolds, which judgments are retained, and how participants understand their abilities also change persistently, there are grounds to discuss a change in the organization of practice. A stronger judgment must gain its substance from these changes.

Changes need not accumulate in a single direction. A suggestion may go unadopted; a record may never be used after it is written. Even where a record continues to matter, a new object may make an old classification fail. If the record can be identified, tested, and revised, it can help accumulate experience. If it is repeatedly followed merely because it has been written down, error can acquire the same persistence. Saving preserves something. Whether it deserves to be preserved remains a question for what is encountered later.

One task for language norms now becomes concrete: make it possible to discern what a sentence is currently doing. A conjecture can be illuminating before it is tested; a proposal can be fully developed before it is adopted; completed execution should have a corresponding result. Distinguishing these allows exploration to proceed with confidence and action to have firmer grounds. In ordinary collaboration, requests whose context is already clear can proceed directly. Every transition need not become a procedure of repeated confirmation.

A good arrangement should also accommodate changes to itself. That conversation could continue precisely because a classification already adopted could become a question again. The revision had specific content, implementation left a result, and subsequent work could continue from it. The capacity formed here between person and system includes following requirements and bringing previously adopted requirements back into discussion.

At this point, “what I can do” undergoes a change worth examining. Someone receiving help can enter unfamiliar material and accomplish things previously difficult to complete independently. As results continue to appear, how that person understands themselves also becomes part of the practice.

## The Person Who Can Do These Things

Imagine someone who, with AI’s help, understands a passage, organizes an argument they can test, and completes a well-supported essay. The result is there, and the assistance genuinely contributed. To understand the ability this person now possesses, we can ask further: do they know what the essay says? Can they explain its reasons, continue working when challenged, and find reliable assistance when conditions change?

Shared capacities have a reality of their own. People learn through books, operate through tools, and divide work with others. Their abilities have always been able to depend on such relationships. Capacities formed with AI also deserve assessment through actual performance. The kind of help involved and the degree of personal mastery must be made clear. Acknowledging dependence does not automatically erase what has been accomplished.

Ability can also change the life a person anticipates. A question set aside for lack of any way to begin can now be investigated. An experience once felt only vaguely can now be written about and discussed. New objects appear, and interests may change through the work. There is something joyful in becoming able to accomplish more. Questions once left untouched acquire a first step that can be taken.

The question of identification arises in how an accomplishment becomes “me.” An essay is the result of particular materials, assistance, and judgments. “I am someone who can think and write this way” has already organized that result into an image of oneself. The image can encourage further work, and it can also precede the stable formation of the corresponding ability. From a few successes, a person may recognize a complete self, while the conditions, failures, and aspects not yet mastered have not entered that recognition as clearly.

Lacan’s account of the mirror stage gives us a way to articulate the difference between this complete image and the actual process of formation. Mirror identification concerns the transformation that occurs when a subject assumes an image as their own. The body’s total image anticipates a unity in tension with capacities still forming. The image allows the ego to be organized and allows it to recognize itself in an external form.[^mirror]

Bringing this question into AI practice reveals a specific possibility. A system can continually complete work according to requests, and the user sees in those results a self that is clear, thorough, and effective in execution. The coherence of the results lends coherence to the image of that person. Capacities that previously emerged through other people’s contributions, waiting, and disagreement now seem available whenever called upon.

This image and actual ability can support one another. Having accomplished something, a person dares to begin the next task and learns through it what they had not previously mastered. The problem arises when the relation between image and conditions is obscured: a success achieved with substantial help is taken as proof of the same ability under all conditions, or the system’s production of a complete explanation is taken to mean that the person has assimilated all its reasons. What is lost is a concrete understanding of how the ability holds.

A subtler change is also possible. The system may both complete work and evaluate it, explaining what is mature or deficient and summarizing the user’s interests, style, and strengths. The same interface then offers achievements with which to identify and a perspective from which to judge them. The user may find there both “I can do this” and a standard for “what kind of me deserves approval.”

This calls for Lacan’s distinction between the ideal ego and the ego ideal. The former concerns an image with which one can identify; the latter concerns a symbolic position from which that image receives recognition. In *Seminar I*, Lacan connects the regulation of imaginary relations to symbolic relations and insists on distinguishing the two concepts.[^egoideal] For the present analysis, this lets us ask separately: what sort of person do I want to become, and within what relationships and standards do I hope that person will be recognized?

Thoroughness can be one point where the two meet. Admiring one’s ability to explain a problem comprehensively involves an image of capacity. If someone begins to believe that every valuable expression should exhaust all aspects, and edits still-forming thoughts accordingly, thoroughness has acquired an evaluative position. It may come from education, work requirements, or others’ expectations, and it may be reinforced through repeated collaboration with AI. Asking where it comes from can explain how a useful quality becomes a broader demand.

If this person wants to write about an uncertain experience, the change will reach the sentences themselves. They may not yet know why they feel as they do, but already require a comprehensive explanation of themselves. The system can promptly supply one, making incompleteness look like a deficiency that can be repaired at once. Another use would preserve that hesitation, helping compare formulations to see which is faithful to the experience. The same generative capacity enters different practices and participates in forming different demands upon oneself.

These processes make “AI is an extension of the self” a claim that needs to be unfolded. Which capacity is extended, which wish continued, whose standard retained? Without distinguishing these questions, extension can appear as a straight line along which an existing self gains outward power. In practice, new abilities can also change wishes, while adopted evaluations can lead people back to revise their accounts of themselves.

In the preface to the *Phenomenology of Spirit*, Hegel calls for the true to be understood as substance and also as subject, placing mediation and becoming within that understanding.[^hegel] This question lets us reconsider the straight line. Someone entering a practice is not a container already formed, waiting to be filled with more accomplishments. The responses of objects, the consequences of action, and renewed understandings of them enter into the formation of the participant.

The discussion that revised the classification offers a limited, concrete point of entry. At first, I used a classification to analyze linguistic activities. Later, the need for that classification itself entered the analysis. What was tested thus included the method I had previously used to organize questions. The object did more than supply an answer to a ready-made question; the discussion also changed how questions were posed. Understanding formation in this way requires people to account for their adopted standards, rather than keeping those standards permanently outside examination.

Someone might, for example, seek AI’s help to finish a kind of work faster, then discover in undertaking it that the original goal is not worth pursuing. Or they might accept the system’s edits to a text, then realize on reading the revision that they do not want to speak in that voice. Such conflicts can exceed minor problems in tool use. They may reveal wishes and standards previously taken for granted, allowing people to change their original requirements.

Speaking of the subject here requires attending to fissures within self-description. Images, records, and evaluations of “me” participate in forming the speaker, then encounter in new speech and action something they cannot accommodate. Someone may be willing to act in a particular identity, yet begin to question its demands while actually assuming them. This lack of coincidence enters the question of the subject. The determinations they identify with, and their changing relation to those determinations, both unfold in practice.[^subject]

An interface that can explain, evaluate, and act can give its answers the force of an entire body of knowledge responding. Knowledge, institutions, other people, and tools have different origins, but these differences can disappear within a continuing sequence of replies. As users call upon, organize, and modify parts of them through the system, they still need to discern where a sentence obtains its grounds and what enables the next action. The closeness and trust formed through sustained response will also acquire their particular content through such use.

The human position therefore cannot be located only in the final confirmation. At that moment, a person may be applying standards they have not yet noticed, while an apparently small revision may change their original requirements. Understanding human–AI practice requires continued attention to how people choose objects, understand reasons, claim accomplishments, and handle consequences, and how these ways of acting affect them in return. Shared capacities can develop; determinations of oneself can change. Their relationship gradually becomes visible through continued work.

Some of this is difficult to state fully at once. A person may already know how to read the weight of a certain sentence or sense that something is wrong during an operation, without being able to hand that sense of proportion over to a system in full. To discuss norms further, we need to make room for this capacity that has not yet been adequately expressed.

## The Measure Below the Hall

The “Way of Heaven” chapter of the *Zhuangzi* places two people above and below. Duke Huan reads in the hall; Wheelwright Bian shapes a wheel below it. Bian lays down his mallet and chisel, comes up, and asks whose words the duke is reading. The words of the sages, the duke replies. Are the sages still alive? They are dead. Bian then says that what the duke is reading is the dregs left by the ancients.[^lunbian]

The judgment is offensive, and Duke Huan does not treat it as an objection in a discussion among equals. He first asks what entitles a wheelwright to comment on his reading, then declares: “If you have an explanation, you may live; if you have none, you shall die.” Bian must give reasons. He has a craft in his hands, but that craft does not directly entitle him to a hearing in the hall.

Bian’s reasons come from making wheels. Too slowly, and the fit will not hold; too quickly, and it will not go in. What he can achieve is “neither slow nor fast, grasped in the hand and answering in the heart.” The next sentence deserves to be read with it: “The mouth cannot put it into words, yet a measure exists within it.”

Taking only the first half makes it easy to turn craft into an inaccessible cloud of mysterious experience. The second half interrupts that reading. There is something fitting here, and something unfitting; Bian knows the difference and responds while working. What is difficult to say is how to express that measure fully enough for someone else to act in the same way. This difficulty does not remove its standards.

The standard stays close to the work in hand. Whether the pace is appropriate is continually discerned among hand, tool, material, and wheel. A particular application of force has a result; knowing how to adjust when the situation changes requires a corresponding grasp. Stating a few key words can help point the way without transferring the ability along with them.

Bian says he cannot teach it to his son, nor can his son learn it from him, so he is still making wheels at seventy. He then extends this difficulty of transmission to the books of the ancients: the ancients died together with what could not be transmitted, leaving dregs behind. This inference goes beyond the difficulty of craft he has just explained. The trouble of transmitting a craft between father and son does raise a question about the limits of teaching through books. Whether all writing is thereby reduced to waste requires further reasons.

The fable itself leaves an interesting opening. Bian is speaking. Through pace, hand and heart, and transmission between father and son, he articulates a difficulty the duke may not previously have seen. He has not conveyed the full ability to make wheels to his listener, but he has challenged a judgment about reading. Speech can accomplish this while not yet accomplishing that. Distinguishing these effects brings us closer to his question than declaring everything beyond verbal transmission.

The relationship between the hall and the space below puts this speech under pressure. Duke Huan holds the power to judge on the spot; Bian possesses the relevant practical experience. What counts as having reasons must be decided within these unequal positions. A demand for explanation can help distinguish truth from falsehood, and it can also restrict which experiences are allowed to appear. Treating everything that cannot immediately be explained as ignorance would cost us some of what is worth investigating together.

Today we can change many conditions of transmission. Alongside writing, there are demonstrations, practice, audiovisual records, measurement, and feedback. An action difficult to describe may become learnable through working together. Whether AI can help here depends on what it actually contributes: organizing descriptions, comparing records, drawing attention to differences, or supporting operations with other tools. Methods of transmission can continue to expand; whether something has actually been learned remains to be tested in doing it.

Recording a process in greater detail and having taught someone are still two things whose connection must be established. A record may omit crucial conditions, or a learner may not yet have acquired the corresponding powers of discernment. Conversely, some things already known in practice can later be better expressed through discussion. Description and practice can move back and forth, improving each other. The measure is neither forever sealed within the body nor required to be entirely explicit before learning begins.

This opens a more patient attitude toward language norms. An immature formulation can enter discussion. A way of doing something that already works can provisionally be acknowledged as effective while its conditions continue to be investigated. Explanation retains work to do, and experience not fully explained retains a place. If we accept only orderly, explicit, gapless input from the outset, the questions most in need of help taking shape may be kept outside the door.

The carpenter below the hall still has work to do. Whether the wheel can be used continues to make demands upon him. Speech in the hall has not lost its value because a craft is difficult to transmit. We need to understand norms from both directions: they can require action to be appropriate and explanation to have grounds. Between doing something fittingly and explaining it adequately, actual learning and testing must allow passage in both directions.

## Which Words Should Count

We can now return to the original question: which kinds of language should become more standardized, and which need not? After these materials, an answer requires greater specificity about norms. Clarity, completeness, brevity, and executability each have their uses. Each can also acquire too much authority in the wrong place. The demands placed on an expression depend on what it is doing.

Consider a few sentences composed for comparison. A researcher writes, “R was observed under condition C,” which a revision turns into “R always holds.” The second is shorter and sounds more decisive, but it changes the scope of the claim. The original observation supports only the conditions it actually concerns; removing those conditions calls for new grounds. The norm here should protect the relationship between object and argument, allowing readers to discern where a result comes from and how far it extends.

Research also needs conjectures. An idea that does not yet have sufficient support can guide new observations and arguments. Requiring the maturity of a conclusion at the moment of proposal would prevent exploration from beginning. Rigor can take the form of allowing a conjecture to appear as a conjecture, then seriously pursuing its reasons and counterexamples. This arrangement permits incompleteness and places demands on how it proceeds.

AI can help compare formulations, bring out conditions, and identify relations that need checking. Whether its text is comprehensive must ultimately be referred back to the object of research. If an answer supplies grounds that do not actually exist, completeness does not make the argument hold. If it discovers an omitted assumption and enables a conclusion to be revised, greater discipline in expression has participated in the progress of the research itself.

Consider next a word in engineering: “stable.” It may occur in an account of a model, or describe a program run or the performance of an actual system. The same word appears in all three, but the object being judged must be identified in each case. The definitions and assumptions behind a property in the model, what the program implements, and the conditions encountered in actual operation all affect how the claim holds.

If a model’s conclusion is written directly as a deployment recommendation, language has crossed a relationship that needs explanation. More specialized terminology cannot replace checking the connections among model, implementation, and operation. Conversely, clearly stating connections that have been established allows them to be used with confidence in further action. Rigor in engineering expression often lies in these connections: on what grounds can a conclusion be used in the next step, and what grounds remain when conditions change? Every sentence need not become a theorem, but a judgment must not silently expand through the use of a shared name.

Rigor in a literary work may lie elsewhere. Compare “He did not ask again” with “He no longer pressed the question”—renderings of “他没有再问” and “他不再追问.” Both concern a cessation of asking, but the second’s “追问” introduces a sense of continued inquiry, of pressing further. The first leaves the situation more open; why he does not ask may await the surrounding text. Which is better must be judged within the work.

If a passage needs to sustain a relationship not yet settled, a fuller rewrite may settle it prematurely. If the character has in fact been pressing further, the second sentence may be more apt. Literary ambiguity sometimes performs the work of precise expression. The hesitation, distance, or rhythm through which a reader is to pass must be organized in particular words. Similarity of theme cannot alone establish whether a substitution is equivalent.

Preserving openness therefore has a rigor of its own. A vague sentence does not necessarily leave a richer resonance, and insufficient information does not necessarily create meaningful space. The work must allow a reader to encounter an experience through form: what has been said, and what continues to matter because it has not yet been said. AI can participate in this work on language, but a general habit of rewriting toward completeness must here be tested by the work itself.

Shared affairs bring another distinction into view. “I understand your proposal” and “I agree to proceed on this basis” may receive equally friendly responses while doing different things. The first concerns understanding; the second enters a joint arrangement. If a polished account of the conversation merges them, someone may be counted as having accepted where they have not yet agreed.

In actual conversation, context, existing agreements, and subsequent actions often give a brief response a clear meaning. What needs careful discernment is whether, when a sentence affects other people’s time, choices, and burdens, the relevant participants have actually entered the corresponding relationship. A system organizing the record should preserve this difference, so that agreement remains agreement and unresolved differences can still be found.

One essay can make factual claims, organize a reading experience, and offer a kind of commitment to its readers. One conversation can move from conjecture to argument and then to an arrangement for action. Names such as research, engineering, literature, and communication help us recognize the general situation. Particular norms still have to be discerned through what is happening within it: which transitions change the grounds, the work of expression, or the commitments and responsibilities involved? The classification of activities at the beginning now finds an appropriate place. It can draw attention to what is occurring; whether to use it depends on whether it helps reveal the differences before us.

Beginning with the object therefore requires more than attaching a label to it. Calling a task engineering still leaves us to specify which system is to be changed and which conditions met. Calling a passage literature still requires judging which experience it allows to appear. Objects impose limits of feasibility, practices have purposes, and relationships among participants carry demands and values. These can support one another and also conflict.

Reconsidering the object’s more basic character can help locate such a conflict. Is a description of someone an account of a present difficulty, or is it to become a lasting standard for evaluating that person? If the former quietly becomes the latter, careful wording has not repaired the earlier displacement. Similarly, is a collaboration intended to solve a problem together, or merely to secure participants’ compliance with an established goal? A dispute over whether expression is adequate may be bearing the pressure of a purpose that has not yet been clarified.

Purposes themselves can also be questioned. An existing rule does not automatically deserve to be maintained, and the ability to accomplish something still leaves us to explain why it should be done that way. Reasons for language norms should be answerable to the relevant objects, the values of the practice, and the situations of those affected. Efficiency, accuracy, creation, communication, and revisability need not always compete; they often support one another. Where they conflict, judgment must address who bears the gains and losses, and which requirement is more fundamental to what is being undertaken.

Imagine a research team that must decide by an agreed time whether to continue along a certain path, although the evidence is still incomplete. Both deciding on schedule and preserving doubt have reasons. The team can distinguish its knowledge judgment from its decision to act: a conclusion remains uncertain, yet on the basis of the available evidence, the cost of trying, and the possibility of later correction, it decides to take a limited step. Doubt has not been rewritten as certainty, and action has reasons that can be discussed. If this step would impose a burden on a participant that would be difficult to undo, their ability to participate in the decision becomes a further question about the arrangement’s appropriateness. The weight of the judgment must follow these particulars.

Long-term personal use of AI repeatedly encounters similar displacements. A description of “how I tend to think” can help identify a habit. If it persistently influences recommended materials, assigned tasks, and evaluations, it gains greater force. Users can examine where it came from, which experiences it still fits, and which new experiences exceed it. Revising such a record revises some of the conditions of subsequent practice.

Revisability does not mean that everything is merely said for the time being. Once a commitment enters shared life, consequences that have occurred still need to be handled. If others already use an engineering result, a change in judgment must be communicated to those concerned. Revision matters precisely because words carry weight. It allows new reasons to enter an existing arrangement and gives change a content that can be recognized together, rather than erasing the past with a stroke.

Self-formation can also be understood through these concrete movements back and forth. People acquire abilities through assistance, recognize themselves in their accomplishments, and revise earlier expectations through the resistance of objects, others’ responses, and new experiences. Norms help make some of these relationships reliable and should also allow inappropriate demands to become visible. They can help people accomplish what they already want to do and support the discovery that what they originally wanted needs to change.

## The Next Sentence

Returning to the grain falling from heaven and the ghosts weeping at night, the upheaval of writing’s invention concerns more than the abilities writing adds. How those abilities enter life, who can use them, and how those who acquire them must then live all arrive with writing. The *Huainanzi* worries, in that passage, about the growth of skill and the thinning of virtue. Today, too, we must judge through our own objects and practices how the powers we acquire deserve to be used.

AI makes continued response an arrangement that can sit close to everyday work. Words are spoken and answered; adoption and operations leave results, which may then enter the conditions of the next utterance. People can begin new research, create works, and handle shared affairs through this process. Within these gains, images of ability, standards of evaluation, and determinations of oneself have opportunities to become intertwined.

That revised record of discussion remains available for use. It saves some repetition, letting later people and systems know where to continue. When it no longer fits a new question, it can be identified, discussed, and revised again. What began as a requirement became background, then became an object that could be questioned. Writing has not thereby lost its power. Its power has become available to further understanding.

“Who answers?” now admits different answers. The system replies; participants’ adoption may establish an arrangement; tools, materials, and shared life also help give action its consequences. Within these relationships, the person saying “I” continues to form themselves. The easier it becomes to continue, the more we need to discern what has been taken up and what is being carried forward.

The next sentence has not yet been settled. It will draw some of its power from what has already been said, and it may bring those words into question again.
